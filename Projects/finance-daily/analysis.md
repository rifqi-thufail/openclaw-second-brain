# Executive Summary

- The Fed is the dominant event of the week and the odds have hardened. Traders price a better than 92% probability of a rate increase at this week's meeting and more than 75% odds of a second hike in December, the first tightening cycle in three years (CNBC Top).
- The long end broke a multiyear barrier. The 10Y US Treasury yield touched 5.01%, its highest since 2007, before buyers pared the move; FT frames it as a level not seen in decades as the Iran-war inflation shock lingers (Straits Times, MarketWatch, FT).

# Top Stories

## Fed hike nearly locked in as Warsh counts the votes | CNBC
Insight: Traders price a better than 92% chance of an increase this week and more than 75% odds of another in December, which makes this the first tightening cycle in three years. A hike itself is nearly priced; the risk is the guidance and the dot path, and MarketWatch notes the Fed historically does not stop at one move.
Action: Reduce duration and crowded high-multiple growth into the decision, hold cash, and treat the statement plus dot path as the single largest risk event for ASEAN rates, the rupiah, and JCI this week.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/counting-the-votes-warsh-faces-tough-battle-as-fed-girds-for-expected-rate-hike.html

## 10Y Treasury yield touches 5.01%, highest since 2007 | Straits Times
Insight: The yield rose almost 5 basis points to as high as 5.01% before paring much of the increase as buyers emerged. MarketWatch notes it briefly hit its highest level since 2007. A 5% risk-free rate compresses equity risk premia globally and drains capital from emerging markets, and it raises the discount rate on every long-duration asset.
Action: Re-anchor valuation work to a 5% risk-free rate. Favour short-duration, cash-generative exposure over long-duration growth, and expect ASEAN equity multiples to face a mechanical de-rating headwind.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/us-10-year-treasury-yield-breaches-5-as-inflation-supply-worries-mount

## Saudi Arabia shuts the East-West pipeline, the main Hormuz bypass | CNBC Asia
Insight: Satellite images confirm damage to the roughly 750-mile pipeline, which Saudi Arabia shut temporarily as attacks threaten the key alternative to the Strait of Hormuz. Removing the primary bypass materially raises market sensitivity to any further Hormuz disruption.
Action: Treat elevated Brent as the base case through winter. Stress-test portfolios for sustained Brent above $110, hedge energy-import exposure, and model renewed fuel subsidy pressure on Indonesia's 2027 budget.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/iran-war-saudi-arabia-east-west-pipeline-oil.html

## AI leaders call for a slowdown; chips become the pain trade | MarketWatch
Insight: Micron, Nvidia and other chip stocks fell after tech leaders called for a slowdown in AI development, and FT reports memory-chip makers and Nvidia slid as warnings combined with rising oil and higher borrowing costs. Chip stocks were the safe AI play and are now the market's pain trade, so the shock hits the crowded, highest-conviction positioning.
Action: Treat AI-infrastructure crowding as the top equity risk for the next month. Trim momentum and index concentration risk, and separate companies with contracted revenue from those priced on AI capex extrapolation.
Source: https://www.marketwatch.com/story/micron-nvidia-and-other-chip-stocks-fall-after-tech-leaders-call-for-an-ai-slowdown-heres-what-to-know-18177588?mod=mw_rss_topstories

Policy is now explicitly pro-AI-capex, which supports data-center, power, and LNG demand even as equity sentiment on AI cools.
Action: Favour the physical infrastructure of AI, power, cooling, and LNG, over AI equity beta. The policy support is a durable demand driver independent of how the chip trade trades week to week.

## US diesel sets a record above $6 a gallon | CNBC Asia
Insight: Diesel surpassed $6 a gallon for the first time ever as the Ukraine and Iran wars tighten supply, and Trump urged Ukraine to stop knocking out Russian refineries. Diesel moves agriculture, mining, and freight, so product cracks, not flat crude, are where the stress is concentrated.
Action: Hedge fuel input costs for industrial, logistics, and airline exposure. Expect another round of fuel surcharges across ASEAN transport, and prefer refining-margin exposure over flat crude beta.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/trump-ukraine-russia-diesel-prices-oil.html

## Vessel struck in the Strait of Hormuz as diplomacy stalls | CNBC Asia

This removes the nearest credible downside catalyst for crude, so the risk premium stays in the price longer than the market assumed.
Action: Do not rely on a quick diplomatic unwind to cap oil. Keep energy hedges in place and favour companies with pricing power over those with unhedged fuel input costs.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: the Jakarta Composite closed at 6,541.38, down 0.73% on the session and down 1.18% on the week, the weakest weekly performance in the ASEAN set. The five-day normalized trend reads 98.8, below the 100 baseline, so the direction is still down, and the index has given back its early-September gains.
- 10Y government bond yield: 7.14%, at the top of the recent range. Direction is flat-to-upward: with the Fed priced near-certain to hike and the US 10Y at 5%, the room for Bank Indonesia to let local yields fall is limited, and any spread widening would push the 10Y up rather than down. Heavier issuance from a new finance minister would add to that pressure.
- 5Y CDS: 83.69 bps, essentially unchanged and near the low end of Indonesia's multi-year range. Direction is stable, but the level is the risk: Indonesia is priced as a low-risk credit while its main trading partners absorb an oil shock and an AI-equity shock, so the CDS has more room to widen from here than to tighten further.
- News that could move the Indonesian market: three threads matter most. First, the fiscal handover: Suahasil Nazara takes the finance ministry in the same week as a near-certain Fed hike and an oil-funded subsidy problem, so his first statements on deficit, subsidy, and bond issuance are a direct JCI and 10Y-yield catalyst. Second, the oil channel: with the Saudis shutting the Hormuz bypass and diesel at a record, Indonesia faces a choice between letting subsidised fuel prices rise into a hawkish-Fed backdrop or absorbing the cost through the budget and higher bond supply. Third, the currency and private capital: the rupiah at 17,630 and the Hartono family's reported $1.4 billion offshore shift are both live signals of external pressure, and with BI having little room to cut, defending the currency would drain reserves.
- Positioning implication: Indonesia screens as a low-beta credit in a high-beta environment. A 7.14% 10Y yield and 83.69 bps CDS leave thin compensation for widening risk, so favour domestically driven, hard-currency-hedged exposure over rate-sensitive and import-cost-exposed names until the Fed and oil paths clarify.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed decision this week is the dominant catalyst: a hike is more than 92% priced, so surprise risk is two-sided but skewed hawkish given firm inflation; a hike plus hawkish guidance pressures the rupiah, JCI, and ASEAN rates simultaneously.
- Oil escalation is the second driver: the Hormuz bypass is shut, a vessel was struck in the strait, Iran says it downed a US drone, and the Gulf-Iran shipping agreement is unsigned, so the risk premium is structurally higher. A Brent move above $110 triggers a second round of imported inflation across ASEAN and renewed subsidy pressure in Indonesia.
- The AI-equity shock is the newest risk: leaders calling for a slowdown, a record diesel print, and 5% US yields land together on the market's most crowded positioning. A deeper chip-market drawdown is the fastest route to a global risk-off impulse that ASEAN cannot decouple from.
- The counter-catalyst is diplomatic and has slipped: an Iran-Gulf shipping agreement on Hormuz remains the fastest way for the risk premium to unwind, but the strike on a vessel ahead of the Oman meeting means the market must wait longer.
- Demand destruction is the self-correcting mechanism: the US consumer outlook at 47.8 (-7.5% month on month, -13.2% year on year), real wages falling at 3.1% growth against 3.4% inflation, and record diesel are early evidence that fuel costs are breaking demand, which would ultimately cap oil and ease the inflation impulse.
- Asia FX stress is the systemic watch item: Japan's record $80 billion reserve drawdown in August after yen intervention is an early warning for carry-trade unwind; watch USD/JPY, regional reserves, and ASEAN FX volatility for the liquidity-squeeze signal.
- Indonesia-specific: the finance minister change adds execution risk to budget and issuance policy in a week already dominated by the Fed and oil; the rupiah at 17,630 and the reported $1.4 billion Hartono-family offshore shift are private and official capital-outflow signals; and rice and food-price dynamics remain an unmodelled subsidy cost.
- Trade policy remains a slow-burn negative: Canada's CA$27.6 billion of retaliatory tariffs and doubled steel and aluminum duties, plus China's soft August import data, keep protectionism and tepid Chinese demand as headwinds for ASEAN external demand into 2027.
- Data quality caveat: VNINDEX failed to fetch entirely, SET and PSEi prints are stale from mid-July, and non-Indonesia CDS values did not return. ASEAN weekly comparisons excluding Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines are the reliable set.
