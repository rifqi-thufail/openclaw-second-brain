# Executive Summary

- Oil broke through $100/bbl: Brent topped $101 on escalating US and Iran strikes around the Strait of Hormuz, closing at its highest since May and reigniting global inflation fears (FT, CNBC, Straits Times).
- US equities fell as the oil shock met rising Treasury yields: S&P 500 -0.48% to 7,636.36, Dow -0.77% to 52,380.66, Nasdaq -0.64%; a Treasury sell-off after Bessent's $6bn buyback disappointed and the 10-year hit a near 3-year high.
- JCI is holding a mild uptrend near 6,650 to 6,686 (latest close 6,686.44, +1.01% on its most recent session), supported by rupiah strength: USD/IDR fell -0.43% on the day to 17,588 and -0.76% on the week, one of Asia's best performers.
- Indonesia 10Y yield held at 7.14% while the 5Y CDS tightened to 82.37 bps from 83.46 a week earlier, a modestly improving sovereign risk signal despite the global bond sell-off.
- ASEAN diverged: Philippine PSEi has been the weakest (sharp -4.24% move earlier in the week) as NPLs climb and 2026 outlook stays pressured; STI, KLCI and SET were softer, tracking US tech and the commodity shock.
- China's August imports missed estimates as pressure builds to rebalance trade, while Japan's foreign reserves fell a record $80bn in August after yen intervention; both feed global growth and currency risk.
- A US and Canada trade war escalated (US import bans on Canadian goods from Sep 29; Ottawa's CA$27.6bn retaliatory tariffs took effect) adding fresh downside to global growth and another inflation vector.

# Top Stories

## Brent crude tops $101, highest in months, as US and Iran trade strikes near Hormuz | CNBC
Insight: Escalating tit-for-tat attacks around the Strait of Hormuz, including reports of hits on tankers and Saudi Aramco-linked facilities, pushed Brent above $101, the highest since May. For import-dependent Asian economies like Indonesia the crude price directly drives fuel subsidy costs and inflation expectations, and it collides with a Fed that is weighing a hike rather than a cut.
Action: Treat Brent above $100 as a shock scenario for IDR assets: expect rupiah and JCI volatility to rise, and hedge commodity-import exposure or overweight energy-linked names in ASEAN portfolios.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-us-iran-hormuz-attacks.html

## Treasury yields jump as Bessent's $6bn buyback plan disappoints | FT
Insight: Upsizing the repurchase programme to $6bn was not enough: the 10-year Treasury yield rose to its highest in nearly three years. The bull market's key risk is now a stronger yen plus higher US yields, a "toxic combination" for equities, and the sell-off raises funding costs for every risk asset including Indonesian sovereigns.
Action: Watch the US 10-year as the global anchor; rising yields argue for shorter duration in fixed income and caution on high-multiple growth equities across ASEAN.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/2476992f-c9fe-4992-887d-f34c81c100ab?syn-25a6b1a6=1

## US unveils import ban on Canadian goods as trade war escalates | CNBC
Insight: Import bans on Canadian motorbikes, alcohol and dairy take effect Sep 29, on top of Ottawa's CA$27.6bn retaliatory tariffs (steel and aluminum duties doubled to 50%) that are already live. A tariff war between two close trading partners adds supply disruption and inflation at the margin, reinforcing the higher-for-longer rate environment.
Action: Do not read the US and Canada spat as contained; it signals protectionism is broadening, which can lift shipping, metals and hedging costs that flow into ASEAN import prices.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/us-canada-trade-war-import-ban.html

## Fed fight comes to a head: Trump pressures Warsh as rate hike looms | CNBC
Insight: Ten days before the September meeting, Waller says he backs holding rates steady while Chairman Warsh leans hawkish and the administration pressures him publicly. With oil back above $100 and diesel at records after Ukraine and Iran refinery knockouts, the inflation data arriving this week could tip an otherwise split committee toward a hike.
Action: Brace for a hawkish surprise; a Fed hike would pressure ASEAN currencies and equities, while an oil-driven hold could still keep rate-cut hopes at bay. Keep duration short and cash dry powder.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/05/trump-warsh-fed-september-rate-hike.html

## Japanese yen strength and Treasury yields seen as the bull market's biggest risk | MarketWatch
Insight: A record $80bn drop in Japan's foreign reserves in August, following joint FX intervention with the US, has pushed the dollar lower against the yen. A stronger yen pressures carry trades and Japanese exporters while higher US yields squeeze global liquidity, a combination that historically marks an equity market top-risk signal.
Action: Monitor USD/JPY alongside the US 10-year as early warning gauges; a sustained yen rally and yield spike argue for trimming leveraged, cyclically sensitive ASEAN exposure.
Source: https://www.marketwatch.com/story/3rd-party-articles/bessent-the-bull-markets-interventions

## BSP chief Remolona earns A- grade; Philippine banks' bad loans rise | Inquirer PH
Insight: Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Governor Remolona kept an A- in Global Finance for managing the inflation and growth trade-off, yet BSP data show NPLs rose in July as borrowers fell behind on credit card and motor vehicle payments under persistently high inflation. Tighter Fed policy plus oil near $100 complicates the Philippines' easing path and keeps the peso and PSEi under pressure.
Action: Stay cautious on Philippine banks and the peso into the Fed decision; the rise in consumer NPLs warrants underweighting consumer finance names until inflation peaks.
Source: https://business.inquirer.net/610105/three-peat-remolona-earns-a-grade-from-global-finance
Source: https://business.inquirer.net/610108/bad-loans-up-amid-continuing-consumer-strain

## China's imports miss estimates as calls for trade rebalancing grow | CNBC
Insight: Exports and imports gathered momentum in August but imports still missed estimates, underscoring weak domestic demand in the world's second-largest economy. For ASEAN commodity and component suppliers, sluggish Chinese demand softens the export outlook even as order books look stable, while Japan's reserve drawdown flags currency tensions among Asian majors.
Action: Prefer ASEAN names with domestic (not China-export) revenue drivers; treat China-sensitive industrials as lower conviction until import demand confirms a turn.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/china-exports-imports-august-trade-rebalance-demand-surplus-.html

- Malaysia: KLCI was little changed near 1,714 on resilient energy and palm names, but the ringgit dipped to a 10-month low against the Singapore dollar, reflecting commodity-import and yield pressure.
- Indonesia: JCI is constructive: rupiah outperformed (-0.43% vs USD on the latest session) and CDS tightened, a positive divergence versus the broader ASEAN risk-off from oil and yields.
- Philippines: Weakest in ASEAN: the PSEi posted a sharp single-session loss and trades near 6,400 in the current snapshot while peso weakness, rising bank NPLs and a possible Fed hike keep 2026 pressure on; Global Finance kept BSP chief at A- on credibility.
- Vietnam: VN-Index data is unavailable this run (symbol fetch failed), but regional news showed the dong's black-market rate rising and a 3-month high in regional inflation on fuel and food costs, a headwind for equities.
- Thailand: SET drifted lower near 1,639 on oil-driven inflation worries; an Indonesian-Thai venture advanced a $1bn, 1,000 MW floating solar project in the Philippines, signaling cross-border green energy capital flows.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI: Holding an uptrend near 6,650 to 6,686; latest close +1.01% on its most recent recorded session with a +1.37% weekly gain, supported by foreign inflows and rupiah strength rather than broad ASEAN risk appetite.
- 10Y government bond yield: Steady at 7.14%, flat versus the prior session and week; the sovereign has so far resisted the global Treasury sell-off, helped by its own demand and a firming rupiah.
- 5Y CDS: 82.37 bps, tightening steadily from 83.46 bps a week ago and 82.54 bps yesterday, signaling improving, low sovereign credit risk even as global rates rise.
- Key market mover: Oil above $100/bbl and the US-Iran Hormuz escalation are the dominant risk to Indonesia. Higher crude enlarges the fuel subsidy bill and stokes imported inflation exactly as BI weighs policy, while a strong rupiah (USD/IDR 17,588, -0.76% on the week) provides BI running room; domestic drivers include a DPR spotlight on Rp71tn in unpaid revenue-sharing transfers to regions with only Rp4tn budgeted for 2027, a fiscal execution concern, and BI-influenced retail and infrastructure signals that should temper the bullish CDS picture with fiscal risk.
- Retail sales and BI policy data arriving alongside ECB and US PPI are the week's domestic catalysts; a hawkish Fed offset would be the main threat to rupiah and JCI.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Oil above $100/bbl rising toward supply-shock levels is the top global and Indonesian risk; each $10 move upstream feeds fuel subsidies, CPI and rate expectations across ASEAN.
- The September Fed meeting is a binary catalyst: Waller wants a hold, Warsh leans hawkish, Trump pressures publicly, and hot oil and diesel data could tip the committee toward a hike that pressures ASEAN FX and equities.
- US and Canada trade escalation (bans and CA$27.6bn tariffs) plus broad trade friction adds an inflation and growth downside vector.
- China's weak import demand and Japan's record $80bn reserve drawdown signal currency and trade stress among Asian majors, a headwind for ASEAN exporters.
- Positive for Indonesia: rupiah outperformance and tightening CDS argue for holding long-IDR, local-bond positions with modest duration; fiscal execution concerns (region transfers) and oil remain the offsets.
- Downside caution: elevated US yields and yen strength together are the classic equity de-rating mix; keep portfolios defensive and rebalance into strength per advisor guidance framed for a bond sell-off and stock boom.
