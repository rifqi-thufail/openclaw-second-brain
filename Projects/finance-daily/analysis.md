# Executive Summary

- Oil surges toward $100/bbl as the US and Iran trade fresh strikes around the Strait of Hormuz, threatening Middle East supply and fanning global inflation risk (FT, CNBC).
- US equities pulled back: S&P 500 -0.58%, Dow -1.18% on AI/software worries, though all three major indices are positive on the week; Dow still above 52,700.
- ASEAN was weak: PSEi (Philippines) plunged -4.22% to 6,133.68, the steepest single-day loss, on peso record lows and rate-hike bets; SET -0.71%, STI -0.68%.
- JCI slipped -0.45% to 6,656.35, but Indonesia's rupiah led Asian currencies higher against the USD, a relative bright spot.
- Indonesia 10Y yield held at 7.14% (flat) while the 5Y CDS tightened to 82.54 bps from 83.46, a mildly improving risk signal.
- China's August CPI/PPI came in near forecasts but PPI topped estimates on commodity costs and tech demand, while weak household demand keeps pressure on Beijing to rebalance.
- Canada-US trade war escalated: Ottawa's CA$27.6bn retaliatory tariffs took effect and Washington banned Canadian dairy, motorcycles and alcohol; watch for ripple effects on global trade and shipping costs.

# Top Stories

## Brent crude nears $100 a barrel as U.S.-Iran tit-for-tat strikes stoke supply worries | CNBC
Insight: Escalating Hormuz attacks (including reported hits on Saudi Aramco-linked infrastructure and tankers) put Brent within touching distance of $100, a level that would reignite global inflation and pressure import-dependent Asian economies including Indonesia, whose fuel subsidy bill rises sharply with crude.
Action: Add energy-cost sensitivity to any long Indonesian rupiah or bond view; expect rupiah and JCI to stay volatile while crude holds above $95, and hedge commodity-import exposure.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-us-iran-hormuz-attacks.html

## Oil nears $100 as US launches new strikes on Iranian tankers | FT
Insight: The US hit multiple Revolutionary Guards-linked ships after further missile attempts on a US Navy warship, and reports suggest Iran launched a second undisclosed attack on US vessels; this is no longer a contained skirmish but a widening supply shock with refinery knockouts (also from the Ukraine war) lifting diesel to records.
Action: Monitor the $100/bbl Brent line as a key trigger; scenario-test higher energy import costs in ASEAN earnings models and consider energy equities as inflation hedges.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/b460b828-6183-4914-9140-5dfb6d61312c

## S&P 500 falls as AI worries hit software makers | Straits Times
Insight: The Dow fell 617.15 points (-1.16%) to 52,797.10 and the S&P slipped 0.58% on AI-valuation concerns, a reversal after a strong early September; US August payrolls of 162,000 (vs 53,000 expected) reduced recession fears but keep the Fed focused on inflation ahead of the September meeting.
Action: Expect ASEAN to mirror US tech/AI sentiment; keep positions nimble into the Fed decision and upcoming US inflation data, and favor quality over high-beta AI names.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/companies-markets/sp-500-falls-as-ai-worries-hit-software-makers

## Shaky peso wobbles to new record low | Inquirer PH / PSEi could end tough 2026 near 6,500

Action: Be cautious on Philippine equities and the peso until the Fed path clarifies; volatility supports defensive/large-cap positioning rather than chasing the dip.

# ASEAN Focus

- Singapore (STI -0.68%): Asia's ultra-rich still favor gold but are branching into digital assets, per Maybank Singapore; lower en-bloc collection-sale thresholds for older estates could revive the property sector (Edge SG).
- Malaysia (KLCI -0.04%): Ringgit weakened notably even as the index held; UOBKH trimmed Delfi's target on higher cocoa prices and forex concerns.
- Indonesia (JCI -0.45%): Rupiah led Asia higher and BI's consumer-confidence index rose in August with stronger spending, offsetting the mild equity pullback.
- Philippines (PSEi -4.22%): Peso at record low on Fed rate-hike bets; Visayas grid on red alert risks more brownouts and higher power rates (~P20/kWh spot).
- Vietnam: Dollar dipped to a 7-month low against the dong; Consumer price inflation hit a 3-month high on fuel and food costs (VnExpress).
- Thailand (SET -0.71%): Sold off with the region; no major idiosyncratic catalyst, tracking global risk sentiment.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI at 6,656.35, -0.45% on the day and -0.17% on the week; mild consolidation after a bounce, tracking softer global tech sentiment.
- 10Y government bond yield steady at 7.14%, unchanged from the prior session, signaling contained local duration pressure so far.
- 5Y CDS improved to 82.54 bps from 83.46 a day earlier, a marginal tightening that points to stable, slowly improving Indonesian sovereign risk.
- Key market mover: rising oil toward $100/bbl is the dominant external risk, lifting Indonesia's fuel-import subsidy burden and stoking inflation expectations; the BI consumer-confidence survey showing stronger August spending suggests resilient domestic demand if energy costs do not translate into fuel-price adjustments.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Oil above $95-100/bbl on US-Iran Hormuz strikes is the single largest tail risk for ASEAN importers and for Indonesian fiscal and inflation dynamics.
- Federal Reserve September meeting (days away) and US CPI this week: any hawkish surprise would lift USD, press Asian FX and equities, and widen EM rate differentials.
- Canada-US trade war escalation ($27.6bn retaliatory tariffs, new US bans on Canadian dairy/motorcycles/alcohol) adds to global trade-fragmentation and shipping-cost concerns.
- China rebalancing pressure and soft household demand: weaker August imports and near-forecast CPI keep export-reliant ASEAN economies exposed.
- UN warning of a supersized El Nino into February 2027 poses a multi-quarter food and energy price risk for agricultural ASEAN economies.
- Vietnam inflation at a 3-month high on fuel/food and dong strength are early signals of regional pass-through from the oil spike.
