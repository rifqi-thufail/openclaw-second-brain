# Executive Summary

- Oil shock intensifies: Brent closed above $101 (highest since May) and US crude topped $100 again as the US and Iran trade strikes around the Strait of Hormuz, with Saudi Aramco facilities reportedly hit (CNBC, FT, Straits Times).
- US equities fell for a fourth straight session: S&P 500 -0.58% to 7,591.70, Dow -0.60% to 52,064.10, Nasdaq -0.65% to 26,081.72; weekly losses of -2.01%, -3.02% and -1.89% respectively.
- US Treasuries sold off hard: the 30-year yield near 5.3% and 10-year at multiyear highs after Bessent's beefed-up buyback underperformed and a weak 30-year auction; the market now prices a 70% chance of a Fed rate HIKE next week (CNBC, MarketWatch).
- JCI held firm: 6,678.20, -0.12% on the latest session and +0.15% on the week, a clear divergence from the global risk-off, supported by rupiah strength.
- Rupiah outperformed: USD/IDR at 17,501, -0.49% on the day and -0.66% on the week, one of Asia's best performers despite the dollar and yield backdrop.
- Indonesia 10Y yield steady at 7.14% and 5Y CDS at 82.68 bps, a low sovereign risk signal that is at odds with the global bond rout but leaves limited room if the Fed hikes.
- ASEAN mixed: PSEi led (+1.25%) on its stale print, SET +0.23%; STI -0.66% and KLCI flat; data gaps persist for Thailand and Philippines index prints.

# Top Stories

## US crude tops $100 and Brent closes above $101 as US and Iran trade strikes near Hormuz | CNBC
Insight: Brent closed at its highest since May, above $101, and WTI topped $100 again as escalating US-Iran hostilities near the Strait of Hormuz raised fears of supply disruption; reports of strikes on Saudi Aramco-linked facilities added to the risk premium. Diesel futures in the US topped $5 a gallon for the first time since 2022. For import-dependent Indonesia this directly raises fuel subsidy costs and imported inflation.
Action: Treat Brent above $100 as the base case for now. Hedge energy-import exposure and expect higher fuel subsidy pressure on the 2027 budget; overweight energy-linked ASEAN names.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/iran-us-oil-hormuz-supply-trump-military-brent-wti.html

## Odds of a Fed rate hike next week jump to 70% | CNBC
Insight: Traders pushed the probability of a September rate increase to 70% in morning action as the oil spike and firm inflation data fed hawkish expectations. PPI rose 0.4% in August as expected. The combination of an oil shock and a Fed tilting to hike is the most hostile mix for long-duration risk assets and for ASEAN currencies.
Action: Position for a hawkish Fed: cut duration, hold cash dry powder and avoid high-multiple growth exposure into the meeting; a hike would pressure the rupiah and JCI even if Indonesia's CDS stays tight.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/the-likelihood-of-a-fed-interest-rate-hike-next-week-just-got-a-lot-higher.html

## Treasury yields surge as Bessent's beefed-up buyback fails to calm the market | MarketWatch
Insight: The Treasury rout intensified after an underwhelming 30-year auction and the first beefed-up buyback operation under Scott Bessent undershot its target; the 30-year climbed to roughly 5.3%, which Jim Cramer flagged as the key force driving equities. A sustained long-end move higher is the classic equity de-rating trigger.
Action: Watch the 30-year yield as the primary risk gauge; a break above 5.3% argues for trimming high-beta ASEAN exposure and shortening bond duration.
Source: https://www.marketwatch.com/story/treasury-yields-surge-toward-the-danger-zone-for-stocks-as-inflation-pressures-heat-up-fe0f9aa6?mod=mw_rss_topstories

## ECB raises rates as Iran war fans inflation fears | Straits Times
Insight: The ECB lifted its benchmark deposit rate to 2.5%, explicitly citing inflation pressures fanned by the Iran conflict, a second major central bank tightening into an oil shock. Nestle's CEO separately said the Middle East conflict is driving inflation and higher supplier costs. This extends the global higher-for-longer regime to Europe.
Action: Do not expect global rate relief; treat the ECB hike as confirmation that oil is being transmitted into core inflation worldwide, which keeps ASEAN central banks (including BI) cautious on easing.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/ecb-raises-interest-rates-to-fight-off-inflation-jump

## Oracle beats on earnings as cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubles | CNBC
Insight: Oracle's results and revenue backlog came in stronger than expected, with cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubling, and its stock edged up. This is a rare positive AI-infrastructure datapoint against a broad tech de-rating driven by higher yields.
Action: Use the beat as evidence that AI capex demand is intact even as multiples compress; favor AI-infrastructure names with hard backlog over speculative AI exposure on any further yield-driven selloff.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/oracle-orcl-q1-earnings-report-2027.html

## China's imports miss estimates as calls for trade rebalancing grow | CNBC
Insight: China's exports and imports gathered momentum in August but imports still missed estimates, underscoring soft domestic demand in the world's second-largest economy as pressure grows to rebalance trade. For ASEAN commodity and component suppliers this softens the export outlook even as order books look stable.
Action: Prefer ASEAN names with domestic revenue drivers over China-export-sensitive industrials until Chinese import demand confirms a turn.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/china-exports-imports-august-trade-rebalance-demand-surplus-.html

## Japan foreign reserves drop a record $80 billion in August after yen intervention | CNBC
Insight: Finance ministry data showed official foreign reserves fell to $1.207 trillion from $1.287 trillion in July, a record monthly drawdown, after joint intervention to support the yen. The scale signals unusual currency stress among Asian majors and points to carry-trade unwinding risk.
Action: Track USD/JPY and Japan's reserve data as an early warning for regional FX dislocation; a stronger yen plus higher US yields is the classic liquidity-squeeze mix for ASEAN risk assets.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/07/japan-foreign-reserves-yen-intervention.html

## US reveals import ban on Canadian goods as trade war escalates | CNBC
Insight: Import bans on Canadian motorbikes, alcohol and dairy take effect September 29, on top of Ottawa's CA$27.6 billion of retaliatory tariffs that doubled US steel and aluminum duties to 50%. A tariff war between two close trading partners adds supply disruption and inflation at the margin.
Action: Do not read the US-Canada spat as contained; broadening protectionism raises shipping, metals and hedging costs that feed into ASEAN import prices, reinforcing higher-for-longer rates.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/us-canada-trade-war-import-ban.html

## Grab in talks to buy Singapore fintech Atome | Straits Times
Insight: Grab is reportedly in talks to acquire Singapore fintech Atome as it seeks growth organically and via acquisitions. The deal would deepen Grab's consumer-credit reach in Southeast Asia and is a signal that regional consolidation in digital finance is accelerating.
Action: Watch Grab and Atome-linked names for event-driven moves; consolidation in ASEAN fintech is a medium-term positive for the sector's scale economics even in a risk-off tape.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/grab-said-to-be-in-talks-to-buy-singapore-fintech-firm-atome

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI: 6,678.20, -0.12% on the latest session and +0.15% on the week, holding a mild uptrend and clearly diverging from the global risk-off driven by oil and US yields.
- 10Y government bond yield: 7.14%, unchanged versus the prior session and week; the sovereign has so far resisted the global Treasury sell-off, supported by firm rupiah demand, but leaves limited buffer if the Fed hikes.
- 5Y CDS: 82.68 bps, a low and stable level consistent with improving sovereign credit risk, though the print is only marginally above last week's 82.37 bps.
- Key market mover: Oil above $100 and the US-Iran Hormuz escalation are the dominant risks: higher crude enlarges the fuel subsidy bill and stokes imported inflation exactly as BI weighs policy, while rupiah strength (USD/IDR 17,501, -0.66% on the week) gives BI running room. A hawkish Fed hike next week is the main threat to the rupiah and JCI; domestic politics and the fiscal execution question remain secondary drivers.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Oil above $100/bbl and rising is the top global and Indonesian risk; each $10 move upstream feeds fuel subsidies, CPI and rate expectations across ASEAN.
- The September Fed meeting is a binary catalyst: markets now price a 70% chance of a hike, and a hike would pressure ASEAN FX and equities including the rupiah and JCI.
- ECB hiking to 2.5% on Iran-driven inflation confirms a synchronised global tightening bias, removing any near-term rate-relief narrative.
- US-Canada trade escalation and broad protectionism add an inflation and growth downside vector that flows into ASEAN import prices.
- China's soft import demand and Japan's record $80bn reserve drawdown signal currency and trade stress among Asian majors, a headwind for ASEAN exporters.
- Positive for Indonesia: rupiah outperformance and a tight 82.68 bps CDS support holding long-IDR and local-bond positions with modest duration; oil and a hawkish Fed are the offsets.
