# Executive Summary

- Risk assets rebounded sharply on 17 September as oil fell for a third session and Treasury yields dipped. The S&P 500 rose 1.14% to 7,637.76, the Nasdaq gained 1.69% to 26,418.30 and the Dow added 0.61% to 51,778.04.
- The bounce is narrow in time, not in breadth. Over the past week the S&P 500 is still down 0.25% and the Dow is down 1.51%, so the move is a relief rally after the Fed's 25bp hike, not a trend reversal.
- Oil is the swing factor. Saudi Arabia is redirecting crude exports through the Strait of Hormuz after the East-West pipeline drone attack, and prices have fallen for three straight days, easing the near-term inflation impulse.
- Asia has not yet participated in the rebound. The JCI closed at 6,436.85 (-0.38% on the day, -2.31% on the week) and the KLCI at 1,679.21 (-1.11%, -2.05%), both lagging the US recovery.
- Indonesia's 10-year government bond yield held at 7.14%, and the 5-year CDS eased to 82.19 bps from 83.74 bps. Improving credit pricing alongside a weakening rupiah remains the core divergence.
- USD/IDR rose to 17,718, up 0.30% on the day and 0.74% on the week, a fourth consecutive session of rupiah weakness.
- Fiscal credibility remains the dominant domestic catalyst after the finance ministry transition, with market attention fixed on the next policy statements and bond auction.

# Top Stories

## Tech leads US stocks to higher close as oil eases, Treasury yields dip | Straits Times
Insight: The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 317.95 points, or 0.62%, to 51,779.85 and the S&P 500 gained 85.93 points, or 1.14%, to 7,637.74, led by technology as energy costs retreated. The rally is mechanically linked to the oil decline: lower crude reduces the inflation impulse that forced the Fed to tighten, which pulls yields down and lifts long-duration growth multiples. Weekly returns remain negative for both indices, which tells you positioning has not shifted, only stress has been released.
Action: Treat this as a tactical bounce inside a higher-for-longer regime, not an allocation signal. Trim into strength on crowded duration-sensitive names, keep the energy overweight but with a tighter stop, and wait for the next CPI print before adding EM beta.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/companies-markets/tech-leads-us-stocks-to-higher-close-as-oil-eases-treasury-yields-dip

## Oil drops for third day as supply concerns ease in Middle East | Edge SG
Insight: Crude fell for a third consecutive session after Saudi Arabia reportedly offered additional supply through the Strait of Hormuz, offsetting the East-West pipeline disruption caused by the drone attack. The US Energy Secretary called the pipeline closure a brief interruption lasting days, but analysts continue to flag a five-to-seven-day inventory cushion, beyond which prices could rally sharply. Trump also said the US is "hopefully" nearing the end of the Iran war, adding a demand-side diplomatic signal.
Action: Fade the geopolitical premium tactically while it unwinds, but keep a cheap upside hedge such as Brent call spreads. For ASEAN, lower oil is a direct positive for Indonesia and the Philippines, both net energy importers, and reduces pressure on fuel subsidy budgets.
Source: https://www.theedgesingapore.com/news/highlight/oil-drops-third-day-supply-concerns-ease-middle-east

## Bank of England defies Fed's rate-hike lead, leaving rates unchanged | CNBC Markets
Insight: The Bank of England held rates steady even after UK inflation rose to 3.1% as energy costs pushed prices higher. This is the first meaningful divergence in the global tightening cycle: the Fed hiked, the BOE paused. A split G10 policy path weakens the dollar's rate differential argument at the margin, which matters for emerging market currencies, including the rupiah, because a softer dollar tone relieves some depreciation pressure.
Action: Watch the dollar index rather than headline Fed rhetoric. If the DXY softens on policy divergence, selective EM local-currency exposure becomes more defensible. Do not extrapolate a BOE pause into a Fed pause; the US CPI path is still the binding constraint.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/bank-of-england-interest-rate-decision-fed-rate-hike-uk-inflation.html

## Trump gains a tariff weapon against China and India. Will he use it? | CNBC Asia
Insight: The US House has paved the way for legislation allowing tariffs of up to 100% on countries buying Russian oil, giving the administration leverage over India and China. For ASEAN, this is a two-sided risk: it could divert Chinese export flows and manufacturing orders toward Vietnam, Malaysia and Indonesia, but it could also slow Chinese demand, which is the region's largest end market. Export-oriented ASEAN economies are directly exposed to any escalation.
Action: Track the legislation's progression and the administration's stated targets. In ASEAN equity portfolios, favour domestic-demand names over export-facing cyclicals until the tariff scope is clear. Vietnam and Malaysia carry the highest sensitivity given their China-linked supply chains.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/trump-russia-sanctions-india-china-oil-tariffs.html

## Singapore's August exports surge by 46% as AI boom powers demand | VnExpress
Insight: Singapore's non-oil domestic exports rose 46% year on year in August, driven by AI-related electronics demand. This is the strongest hard-data confirmation yet that the AI capital expenditure cycle is still accelerating rather than peaking. It contrasts sharply with the soft consumer and transport data coming out of the US and Europe, and it partially explains why the STI has held up better on a relative basis despite the global duration shock.
Action: Keep exposure to AI supply-chain beneficiaries, particularly semiconductor, test equipment and data-centre linked names in Singapore, Malaysia and Taiwan. Treat AI capex as the most durable growth engine in the region and prefer it over consumption-led recovery trades.
Source: https://e.vnexpress.net/news/business/economy/singapore-s-august-exports-surge-by-46-as-ai-boom-powers-demand-5121261.html

## Indonesia's new finance minister faces an uphill battle on fiscal credibility | CNBC Markets
Insight: Nazara was sworn in hours after Purbaya's dismissal. He spent seven years as deputy finance minister and led the ministry's fiscal policy agency from 2015 to 2019, so institutional knowledge is deep, but an abrupt transition at the top of the finance ministry always raises questions about policy continuity and the credibility of the deficit path. Indonesian markets are still waiting for his first substantive policy signal, and the rupiah at 17,718 reflects that uncertainty.
Action: Do not add Indonesian duration until the fiscal framework is publicly reaffirmed. Watch the next government bond auction for bid-to-cover and the first policy statements for guidance on the deficit ceiling and tax reform sequencing. A weak auction would validate the rupiah's depreciation trend.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/indonesia-finance-minister-msci-prabowo-.html

## Semirara uncertainties deepen as DOE scraps 2026 coal auction | Inquirer PH
Insight: The Philippine Department of Energy has scrapped the 2026 coal auction, deepening uncertainty around Semirara Mining and Power, the country's largest coal producer. The decision removes a visible medium-term growth avenue for the company and signals a policy shift in Philippine energy planning. Coal remains a material share of the Philippine power mix, so the supply-side implications for domestic electricity prices are also relevant to the inflation outlook.
Action: Reduce or avoid exposure to Philippine coal-linked equities until the DOE clarifies its 2026-2027 upstream plan. Factor potential electricity price effects into Philippine inflation forecasts, since higher power costs would complicate any BSP easing path.

# ASEAN Focus

- Singapore: The STI closed at 5,635.41, down 0.06% on the day and 0.96% over the week. The August non-oil domestic export surge of 46% year on year, powered by AI demand, is the standout regional data point and provides a strong fundamental floor. The Singdollar recovered against the US dollar after initially weakening on the Fed hike, per Straits Times. A Bank of Singapore note highlighted that only 22% of Singapore-listed companies disclose physical climate risk in detail, an emerging governance gap that could affect index inclusion and capital costs over time.
- Malaysia: The KLCI fell 1.11% to 1,679.21, and is down 2.05% over the week, one of the weaker ASEAN prints. There is no fresh index-relevant catalyst in today's feed beyond the broad regional risk-off and the tariff-leverage story, which matters for Malaysia given its position in China-linked electronics supply chains. Treat the KLCI as a China-beta proxy for now and watch for follow-through on the US tariff legislation.
- Indonesia: The JCI fell 0.38% to 6,436.85 and is down 2.31% on the week. The 10-year yield held at 7.14% and the 5-year CDS improved to 82.19 bps, but the rupiah weakened to 17,718. See the Indonesia Focus section for the full read.
- Philippines: The BSP is expected to take a measured approach to rate policy despite the Fed's hike, with analysts warning that aggressive tightening would unsettle markets and add financial volatility (Inquirer). The DOE's decision to scrap the 2026 coal auction deepens uncertainty for Semirara. Separately, the government approved changes to the North-South railway and Mactan-Cebu airport projects, keeping the infrastructure pipeline active, and approved over 26,000 new DSWD posts. Soft demand is expected to slow Toyota's Philippine production in 2026, and meat traders are pushing back on higher pork jowl tariffs, both signs of uneven domestic demand.
- Vietnam: The State Bank of Vietnam's exchange rate hit another peak and the dollar strengthened against the dong on the black market, confirming broad currency pressure across the region. Gasoline prices reached a four-month high, a direct hit to domestic purchasing power and transport margins. On the structural side, a forecast places Vietnam as the fastest-growing of Southeast Asia's six largest economies, with the group expected to expand 4.8% annually through 2035, and Southeast Asian investors have become the largest foreign buyers of Hong Kong commercial property, a sign of regional capital export capacity.
- Thailand: The SET index was dropped by the data provider this run due to insufficient data, so no directional call is made. The same gap applies to the Philippines' PSEi and Vietnam's VN-Index in this briefing's quantitative section. Treat the absence as a data gap rather than a neutral signal, and rely on the qualitative ASEAN bullets above for those markets.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: The JCI closed at 6,436.85, down 0.38% on the day and down 2.31% over the week. The normalized five-day series has fallen from 100.0 on 10 September to 97.7 on 16 September, a persistent one-way decline that shows steady distribution rather than a single-day shock. Indonesia is among the weakest of the tracked ASEAN indices on a weekly basis.
- 10Y government bond yield direction: The 10-year Indonesian government bond yield stands at 7.14%, unchanged from the prior session. The flat print is notable given the Fed's 25bp hike and the US 10-year's elevated level, and it suggests domestic demand is still absorbing supply. The risk case is that a sustained rise in US yields forces repricing at the next auction; the base case is stability until the new finance minister's policy signal lands.
- 5Y CDS level and direction: The 5-year Indonesia CDS is 82.19 bps, down from 83.74 bps in the prior session. The direction is positive and the absolute level remains low, which means foreign credit markets are not pricing a fiscal credibility problem. The gap between improving CDS and a weakening rupiah is the key divergence to monitor; if CDS starts rising while the rupiah falls, that would signal a genuine stress repricing.
- Rupiah: USD/IDR closed at 17,718, up 0.30% on the day and 0.74% over the week. The five-day normalized series rose from 100.0 to 100.7, a persistent grind weaker rather than a break. Continued depreciation combined with firm US yields is the most direct transmission channel into foreign equity outflows from the JCI.
- News that could move the Indonesian market significantly: The finance ministry transition remains the dominant domestic catalyst, with market attention fixed on Nazara's first policy statements and the next bond auction. Oil's three-day decline is a material offsetting positive for Indonesia's external balance and fuel subsidy budget, and would reduce pressure on the rupiah if sustained. The US tariff legislation targeting buyers of Russian oil carries indirect risk for Indonesian commodity exports if it broadens. Regionally, the yen and Japanese rate path remain a flow risk for Indonesian bonds given Japanese institutional participation in emerging market credit.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed follow-through: The Fed hiked 25bp and markets had priced a further move in December. A second consecutive hike would extend US yields higher and pressure Indonesian bonds, the rupiah and EM equity multiples simultaneously.
- Oil reversal: The three-day oil decline depends on Saudi supply rerouting through Hormuz and a pipeline restart within days. The inventory cushion is only five to seven days. A failure to restart would reverse the move sharply and reintroduce the inflation impulse for Indonesia and the Philippines in particular.
- Indonesia fiscal credibility: The finance ministry transition is unresolved at the policy level. The next bond auction and Nazara's first statements are the key tests. A credibility loss would show up first in the rupiah and then in CDS, which is currently the more benign of the two signals.
- Rupiah depreciation: USD/IDR at 17,718 with a fourth consecutive session of weakness is a slow bleed. Faster depreciation would force Bank Indonesia into defensive rate action, conflicting with the growth agenda and with the government's spending plans.
- US tariff legislation: The House's move to enable up to 100% tariffs on buyers of Russian oil creates a new trade-policy risk for China and India, with knock-on effects on ASEAN export demand and supply-chain reconfiguration.
- Policy divergence risk: The BOE's decision to hold while the Fed hikes signals a fractured global policy path. Divergence can weaken the dollar at the margin, which helps EM currencies, but it can also signal that growth expectations outside the US are deteriorating.
- Regional corporate and sector risk: The Philippine coal auction cancellation leaves Semirara's medium-term outlook unclear, and soft demand is cutting Toyota's Philippine production plans. These are idiosyncratic but they add to the sense that domestic demand across ASEAN is uneven.
- Business cycle risk: The AI capex cycle, confirmed by Singapore's 46% export surge, remains the strongest growth engine, but it is concentrated. A slowdown in AI-related semiconductor demand would hit Singapore, Malaysia and Vietnam disproportionately.
