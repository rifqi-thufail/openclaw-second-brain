# Executive Summary
- Global risk appetite stayed firm on Friday: Nikkei +4.03%, Nasdaq +1.00%, S&P 500 +0.70%. Singapore STI was the outlier at -0.79%, pressured by the MAS tightening surprise.
- The Fed is the key variable. Kevin Warsh faces a divided committee, with dissenters pushing hikes while June core inflation holds at 3.3%. J.P. Morgan now models a more hawkish path.
- Energy is the new macro shock. Brent is back above USD 90 as Iran war risk disrupts Strait of Hormuz shipping. Exxon, Chevron and Shell reported surging profits.
- Singapore's surprise monetary tightening, on oil-driven inflation, is a signal for ASEAN central banks: the pass-through of energy costs is back on the agenda.
- Indonesia: JCI rose +0.80% to 6,236.13. The 10Y govt bond yield is elevated at 7.14% and 5Y CDS is tight at 93.36 bps, showing firm fiscal confidence despite rate pressure.
- China's factory PMI unexpectedly contracted in July on weak demand and typhoon disruption. This is a drag for ASEAN exporters and commodities.
- Policy FX is active: the US Treasury intervened to support the yen, with Tokyo confirming joint action. Expect more volatility in USD/JPY.

# ASEAN Focus
- MAS tightening is the region's most important policy signal this week. It raises the bar for Bank Indonesia and BSP to tolerate currency weakness.
- JCI +0.80% and KLCI +0.26% held up, supported by commodity prices. Indonesia's coal and palm sectors benefit from energy inflation.
- ASEAN integration push (DPM remarks) supports structural FDI themes: digital economy, data centers, supply chain relocation.
- Watch: oil importers (PH, TH) face current account pressure if Brent stays above USD 90.

# Indonesia Focus
- JCI at 6,236.13 (+0.80%) leads ASEAN this session. Momentum is constructive but the market remains rate-sensitive.
- 10Y govt bond yield at 7.14%, the highest since April 2025, with 20 bps added over 4 weeks. The yield curve is pricing BI policy caution.
- 5Y CDS at 93.36 bps is stable and low by EM standards, indicating limited sovereign credit stress. Monitor direction: a break above 100 bps would signal investor concern.
- News to watch: Bank Indonesia rate decision language, rupiah weakness vs USD (fifth consecutive monthly loss risk), and oil-driven inflation pass-through. Any upside CPI surprise would pressure bonds and equities simultaneously.

- Downside: Fed hike surprise, Strait of Hormuz closure, China PMI slide spreading to ASEAN exports, tariff escalation on generic drugs from 2028.
- Catalysts this week: US CPI, Fed speakers, China trade data, ASEAN Q2 GDP prints, Bank Indonesia policy signals.
