# Executive Summary

- US equities closed a volatile week with a modest recovery. The S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq added 0.39% to 26,522.54 on 18 September, while the Dow slipped 0.18% to 51,682.64.
- The weekly picture is less constructive than the daily print. The S&P 500 is up just 0.40% over the week and the Dow is down 1.41%, confirming a narrow, technology-led tape rather than broad risk appetite.
- The Fed remains the dominant macro variable after Kevin Warsh's first hike as chair. Markets are debating how far the tightening goes next, and the bond market's reaction to his remarks has been the key price signal.
- Oil finished the week flat. Saudi Arabia restarted the damaged East-West pipeline within days and offered additional crude through Hormuz, so the supply shock premium unwound without breaking the inflation impulse.
- Asia diverged from the US. The JCI closed at 6,462.43 (+0.40%), the STI at 5,660.52 (+0.45%), the Nikkei at 64,136.25 (+0.33%), while the Hang Seng fell 0.44% and the KLCI lost 0.27%.
- Indonesia's external indicators are pulling in opposite directions. The 10-year government bond yield held at 7.14%, the 5-year CDS eased to 81.78 bps, but USD/IDR climbed to 17,740, up 0.62% on the week.
- The Philippine PSEi broke below 5,900 (-1.72% to 5,855.91), the weakest ASEAN print in this run, as rising yields, oil and peso weakness hit domestic appetite.

# Top Stories

## S&P 500, Nasdaq advance, turning the page on a tumultuous week | Straits Times
Insight: The S&P 500 gained 12.74 points, or 0.17%, to 7,650.50 while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64. The divergence is the story: gains are concentrated in technology and AI-linked names, while cyclicals and industrials lag. A 0.40% weekly gain for the S&P 500 after a Fed hike tells you the market is treating tightening as priced rather than incremental.
Action: Keep portfolio beta in quality growth with visible AI-linked revenue, and avoid broad cyclical reflation trades until the September CPI print confirms the inflation trajectory. Use the Dow's relative weakness as a signal on industrial and consumer exposure.

## Three words from Kevin Warsh have Wall Street wondering how far the Fed will go with rate hikes | CNBC Top
Insight: Chair Warsh used his first post-decision remarks to justify this week's rate increase while leaving the forward path open, and the phrasing has re-opened the debate on the terminal rate. The bond market initially welcomed the tone, but the long end is still expected to trade at elevated yields. This is the single largest transmission channel into emerging market asset prices, including Indonesian bonds and the rupiah.
Action: Trade the front end on data, not rhetoric. Hold duration short in US Treasuries, and in EM local debt prefer markets with improving credit (Indonesia CDS at 81.78 bps) over those with current-account deficits. Reassess after the next CPI release.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/three-words-from-kevin-warsh-have-wall-street-wondering-how-far-the-fed-will-go-with-rate-hikes.html

## Oil finishes the week flat as the market sees Saudi pipeline outage as less disruptive than feared | CNBC Asia
Insight: Crude ended the week broadly unchanged after Saudi Arabia reportedly offered more supply through the Strait of Hormuz and the US said the damaged East-West pipeline would restart within days. Markets shrugged off continued Saudi-Houthi cross-border strikes, which signals the geopolitical risk premium is being priced as temporary rather than structural. For ASEAN, flat-to-lower oil directly relieves fuel subsidy budgets in Indonesia and reduces the Philippines' import bill.
Action: Keep a small, cheap upside hedge in place because the supply cushion remains thin, but do not underwrite sustained triple-digit crude. In regional equities, treat lower oil as a positive for Indonesian and Philippine domestic demand names and a mild negative for upstream energy earnings.

## PSEi sinks below 5,900 on rising yields, oil prices | Inquirer PH

ANZ separately warned that Asian economies with wide current-account deficits and limited AI-boom benefit, such as the Philippines, are most sensitive to expectations of higher US rates. The Philippines is now the clearest demonstration of how the Fed's tightening transmits into ASEAN risk assets.
Action: Underweight Philippine equities versus Indonesia, Singapore and Vietnam until the peso stabilises and the BSP's response is clarified. Watch the BSP's next policy statement for whether it defends the currency or prioritises growth: a defensive hike would pressure domestic cyclicals further.

## Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire chair, ending six-decade run | Straits Times
Insight: Buffett handed the Berkshire chair to his son Howard after building a failing textile mill into a US$1.1 trillion conglomerate. This is a governance milestone for the largest US conglomerate: succession removes the key-man premium that has historically supported the stock's valuation multiple and shifts the market's attention to capital allocation under the successor structure. Berkshire's cash balance and buyback policy become the swing factor for the shares.
Action: Expect near-term volatility in Berkshire and in the valuations of companies where Buffett's stake is read as a quality endorsement. Do not treat the announcement as a broader market signal; company-specific succession risk is contained to the issuer and its closest comparables.
Source: https://www.straits.com/business/companies-markets/warren-buffett-steps-down-hands-berkshire-chair-post-to-son

## OpenAI's listing delay raises stakes for SoftBank's $50bn data centre IPO | FT Home
Insight: A delay to OpenAI's own listing increases pressure on SoftBank's SB Energy data centre IPO, and SoftBank separately added US$21 billion of fresh debt capacity including a larger Arm margin loan. This is the AI capital cycle's funding stress point: equity markets are being asked to absorb increasingly large infrastructure issuance while the anchor demand story stays private. Oracle's US$18 billion data centre debt is also under strain from permitting and construction delays in New Mexico.
Action: Treat AI infrastructure credit as the emerging risk concentration of the cycle. Prefer AI supply-chain equity exposure (semiconductors, test equipment, data-centre operators with contracted revenue) over unsecured data-centre debt, and monitor the SB Energy IPO outcome as a sentiment marker for the whole complex.

## Indonesia's new finance minister faces an uphill battle on fiscal credibility | CNBC Markets
Insight: The abrupt transition at the top of Indonesia's finance ministry leaves the deficit path and policy continuity unresolved, and the market is waiting for the first substantive policy signal. The rupiah at 17,740, a fifth consecutive session of weakness, is the clearest expression of that uncertainty. Crucially, the 5-year CDS eased to 81.78 bps while the currency weakened, which means foreign credit markets are not yet pricing a fiscal credibility problem.
Action: Do not add Indonesian duration until the fiscal framework is publicly reaffirmed. Watch the next government bond auction for bid-to-cover and foreign participation, and read the first policy statements for guidance on the deficit ceiling. A weak auction would validate further rupiah depreciation; a strong one would make the current yield level attractive.

Financials are the live theme: Macquarie Capital upgraded UOB after a detailed review of its ASEAN quality and valuation, and OCBC reported that one in four of its blue-chip investment plan customers is now under 35, a structural retail-flow positive. Grab's US$1.9 billion Atome acquisition advances its ASEAN super-app strategy, but the price paid is the open question.
- Malaysia: The KLCI fell 0.27% to 1,674.74 and is down 1.80% over the week, making it the weakest of the tracked ASEAN indices on a weekly basis. There is no fresh index-level catalyst in the feed; the market remains a China and electronics-cycle beta proxy. EcoWorld's award of a Singapore GLS site signals continued cross-border capital deployment by Malaysian developers.
- Indonesia: The JCI rose 0.40% to 6,462.43, snapping a three-session decline, though it remains down 1.21% on the week. Yield and CDS are stable-to-improving while the rupiah continues to grind weaker. See the Indonesia Focus section for the full read.
- Philippines: The PSEi fell 1.72% to 5,855.91, breaking the 5,900 level. ANZ flagged the Philippines alongside other current-account-deficit economies as most exposed to a hawkish Fed. On the primary market side, the P92 billion GCash IPO is set for October, which will test institutional appetite for Philippine risk at a difficult macro moment. The PSE is also pushing criteria for a green equity label and the ERC is reviewing the 50-kilowatt power retail threshold, both structural market-development items.
- Vietnam: The dong firmed slightly against the dollar on Friday, easing the recent depreciation pressure, while domestic gold prices rose on global bullion strength. Foreign direct investment appetite remains strong: Central Group, controlled by Thailand's Chirathivat family, is considering a US$1.5 billion Vietnam investment, and Meey Global filed publicly for a US IPO. Separately, Hong Kong edged ahead of Singapore in the global financial centre ranking, a competitive marker for regional capital flows.
- Thailand: The SET index was skipped again this run because of insufficient price data, so no quantitative call is made. The same gap applies to the Philippines' PSEi and Vietnam's VN-Index in the tables and charts. Treat the absence as a data gap, not a neutral signal. # Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: The JCI closed at 6,462.43, up 0.40% on the day, ending a three-session run of declines (6,541.38 on 15 September, 6,534.69 on 16 September, 6,461.15 on 17 September, 6,436.85 on 18 September). The index is still down 1.21% over the week and has not recovered the level seen a week ago. The bounce is a stabilization, not a reversal; sustained recovery requires foreign flows to return, which depends on the currency.
- 10Y government bond yield direction: The 10-year Indonesian government bond yield stands at 7.14%, unchanged from the prior four sessions (7.14% on 15, 16, 17, 18 and 19 September). A completely flat yield through a week that included a US Fed hike is the strongest evidence that domestic demand is absorbing supply and that the market is not repricing Indonesian credit risk. The risk case remains a delayed repricing: if US long-end yields extend higher, the next auction clears at a higher yield.
- 5Y CDS level and direction: The 5-year Indonesia CDS is 81.78 bps, down from 82.19 bps the prior session and 83.74 bps a week ago. The direction is consistently positive (83.69, 84.48, 83.74, 82.19, 81.78 over the last five sessions) and the absolute level is low by historical EM standards. Foreign credit markets are not pricing a fiscal credibility problem. The divergence between improving CDS and a weakening rupiah is the key signal to monitor: if CDS turns higher while the rupiah keeps falling, that would be a genuine stress repricing rather than a liquidity-driven currency move.
- News that could move the Indonesian market significantly: The finance ministry transition remains the dominant domestic catalyst. Market attention is fixed on the new minister's first substantive policy statements and the next government bond auction for bid-to-cover and foreign participation. Oil's flat weekly finish is a material offsetting positive for Indonesia's external balance and fuel subsidy budget. The US tariff legislation enabling duties on buyers of Russian oil carries indirect risk for Indonesian commodity exports if the scope broadens, and Prabowo's public confrontation with economists adds a political noise factor that could affect policy signalling credibility.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed follow-through: Chair Warsh's remarks left the tightening path open. A second consecutive hike would lift US yields, pressure Indonesian bonds and the rupiah simultaneously, and compress EM equity multiples. This is the highest-probability systemic risk in the current setup.
- Rupiah depreciation: USD/IDR at 17,740 with a fifth consecutive session of weakness and a 0.62% weekly rise. The CDS-yield-rupiah divergence cannot persist indefinitely; if the currency breaks materially faster, Bank Indonesia faces a choice between defensive rate action and the government's growth agenda.
- Indonesia fiscal credibility: The finance ministry transition is unresolved at the policy level. The next bond auction and the new minister's first policy statements are the tests. Loss of credibility would show first in the rupiah, then in CDS.
- Oil reversal: The supply cushion remains thin despite the pipeline restart and Saudi rerouting through Hormuz. Renewed Saudi-Houthi escalation or a failed restart would revive the inflation impulse and hit Indonesia and the Philippines hardest.
- AI infrastructure funding: OpenAI's listing delay, SoftBank's US$21 billion of new debt, Oracle's US$18 billion data-centre debt strain and OpenAI's projected US$280 billion cash burn to 2030 are the same risk from different angles. A funding squeeze in AI infrastructure would hit Singapore, Malaysia and Vietnam supply chains disproportionately and would be the first cyclical crack in the strongest regional growth engine.
- Philippine currency and equity risk: The PSEi below 5,900 with a weak peso and elevated oil prices. ANZ explicitly ranks the Philippines among the most Fed-sensitive Asian economies. A disorderly peso move would force BSP action and pressure domestic demand.
- US trade policy: The House's move to enable tariffs of up to 100% on buyers of Russian oil gives the administration new leverage over China and India, with knock-on effects on ASEAN export demand and supply-chain reconfiguration.
- Data gaps: The SET (Thailand), PSEi (Philippines) and VN-Index (Vietnam) indices were skipped due to insufficient price data, and ASEAN CDS peers (MY, TH, PH, VN, SG) remain unavailable. Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines are therefore covered qualitatively only in the quantitative sections.
