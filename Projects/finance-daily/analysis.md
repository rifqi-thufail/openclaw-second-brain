# Executive Summary

- US equities bounced on Friday but lost the week: S&P 500 +0.86% to 7,656.98 (-0.80% wk), Nasdaq +0.96% to 26,333.04 (-0.66% wk), Dow +0.98% to 52,573.29 (-1.57% wk). The rally is a single-input relief trade on a softer oil print, not a change in trend.
- The Fed is the dominant catalyst this week. August core CPI topped forecasts, headline ran 3.4% year on year against 3.1% wage growth, and traders pushed September hike odds to about 70%, the first tightening in three years (CNBC, Straits Times, Edge SG, MarketWatch).
- Oil is the transmission channel from geopolitics to inflation. US crude topped $102 on Thursday, its highest close since May, then gave back part of the move into Friday on hopes of a diplomatic opening. CNBC Indonesia puts Brent-linked pricing at US$107 after the Saudi pipeline shutdown (CNBC Asia, CNBC Indonesia).
- Supply risk is structural, not headline. Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline, the main Hormuz bypass, after drone attacks from Iraq; the Houthis advanced to a Red Sea island near Bab el-Mandeb; and the Gulf states postponed their Iran-Hormuz meeting for lack of consensus (CNBC, FT, Edge SG).
- Product markets carry the acute stress. US diesel set a record above $6 a gallon and the IEA warned the refining system is "stretched to the limit", which is the channel that turns a crude headline into core goods inflation (CNBC).
- Asia sold off hard into the oil and yield shock and only partly recovered: Nikkei 225 -1.93% on Friday to 64,011.34 (-3.60% wk), Hang Seng -0.60% to 24,805.63 (-2.39% wk), KLCI -1.10% to 1,686.74. STI was the relative hold at +0.11% but still -1.66% on the week (Market data).
- JCI was the ASEAN laggard: 6,541.38, -0.73% on the session and -1.18% on the week. Indonesia's risk pricing stays benign but offers no compensation: 10Y yield 7.14% (flat), 5Y CDS 82.85 bps (flat), rupiah soft at 17,606 (Market data).

# Top Stories

## Fed hike odds near 70% after firm core CPI | CNBC
Insight: August core CPI came in above the 0.2% consensus and headline ran 3.4% year on year against 3.1% wage growth, so real incomes are falling and the Fed sees a case for tightening. Traders pushed September hike odds to about 70%, the first increase in three years, and MarketWatch notes the Fed historically does not stop at one.
Action: Cut duration and crowded high-multiple growth into the meeting, hold dry powder, and treat the Fed statement and dot path as the single largest risk event for ASEAN risk assets and the rupiah this week.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/cpi-inflation-report-august-2026.html

## Oil prices jump again as Saudi Arabia shuts the East-West pipeline | CNBC Asia
Insight: Drone attacks from Iraq forced Saudi Arabia to shut the East-West crude pipeline, the main route that avoids the Strait of Hormuz, and crude rose on the news into Sunday night. Removing the primary bypass materially raises market sensitivity to any Hormuz disruption, and CNBC Indonesia reports world oil at US$107.
Action: Treat Brent above $100 as the base case. Stress-test portfolios for sustained Brent above $110, hedge energy-import exposure, and model renewed fuel subsidy pressure on Indonesia's 2027 budget.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/13/oil-price-iran-war-strait-hormuz-saudi-pipeline.html

## Gulf states postpone the Iran talks on Hormuz | FT
Insight: Gulf states delayed a crunch meeting with Iran over the Hormuz impasse because of a lack of consensus, setting back efforts to restore shipping through the waterway. This removes the nearest credible downside catalyst for crude, so the risk premium stays in the price for longer than the market assumed on Friday.
Action: Do not rely on a quick diplomatic unwind to cap oil. Keep energy hedges in place and favour companies with pricing power over those with unhedged fuel input costs.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/4845a503-3ddd-4bb7-a469-c51f275bc873

## US diesel hits a record above $6 a gallon as refining stays stretched | CNBC
Insight: Diesel, the fuel that moves agriculture, mining, and freight, set an all-time record, and the IEA warned inventories are shrinking and refineries are "stretched to the limit" as the Iran and Ukraine wars tighten supply. Product cracks, not flat crude, are where the stress is concentrated.
Action: Hedge fuel input costs for industrial and logistics exposure, expect another round of fuel surcharges across ASEAN transport and airlines, and prefer refining-margin exposure over flat crude beta.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/diesel-price-iran-strait-hormuz-ukraine-russia.html

## StanChart warns oil is now built for sharper, more frequent spikes | Yahoo Finance
Insight: StanChart warns the oil market structure now produces sharper and more frequent price spikes, and a separate Yahoo Finance piece notes further spikes could rekindle recession fears. With the Hormuz bypass shut and Red Sea shipping under threat, the distribution of oil outcomes has fattened on the upside.
Action: Size positions for higher realised volatility rather than a higher average price. Use options to cap tail cost instead of adding linear energy length, and monitor recession-fear headlines as the signal that demand destruction is taking over.
Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/stanchart-warns-oil-now-built-230000074.html

## Consumer outlook plunges in September as the inflation outlook worsens | CNBC
Insight: The consumer outlook index fell to 47.8, down 7.5% from August and 13.2% from a year ago, with sentiment sliding on higher gas prices and trade tensions. Demand destruction from fuel costs is now visible in the data, which is the mechanism by which an oil shock eventually caps itself.
Action: Watch ASEAN consumer discretionary, travel, and transport exposure for downgrade risk. The demand-destruction signal is early, so keep positioning ahead of it rather than reacting after earnings cuts.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/consumer-outlook-plunges-in-september-as-inflation-outlook-worsens.html

## World oil tops US$107 as the Saudi pipeline closes | CNBC Indonesia
Insight: CNBC Indonesia reports world oil at US$107 after the Saudi pipeline shutdown, with Middle East tensions and drone attacks adding to global supply concerns. For Indonesia this flows straight into subsidised fuel cost, imported inflation, and the credibility of the 2027 budget assumptions.
Action: Model Pertamina and subsidy cost at Brent above $100 for the remainder of 2026. Favour energy-linked Indonesian names and hard-currency earners, and stay selective on transport and consumer discretionary.
Source: https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/market/20260914062859-17-767559/jalur-pipa-saudi-ditutup-harga-minyak-dunia-tembus-us-107

## Rate-hike fever spreads across G-7 central banks | Straits Times
Insight: Attention is on Warsh after the stronger-than-expected core inflation reading on Sept 11, and hawkish repricing is spreading across G-7 central banks while the ECB's Lagarde says the current inflation shock will be longer lasting. Synchronised tightening is the worst configuration for a small open economy with a soft currency.
Action: Expect ASEAN central banks to face a weaker case for easing and a stronger case for holding or hiking. Trim duration in local-currency ASEAN bonds and favour floating-rate or short-dated exposure.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/all-eyes-on-warsh-as-rate-hike-fever-spreads-across-g-7-central-banks

## Trump escalates pressure on Warsh as the Fed hike looms | CNBC
Insight: Ten days before the Fed meeting, the administration is in a full-court press to stop a hike, and Warsh faces intensifying pressure after hot CPI. Political pressure on the Fed raises the risk of a credibility shock if the committee hikes anyway or signals more to come.
Action: Price a small probability of a policy-credibility event into long-end US Treasury and USD positioning, and note that a hawkish Fed that defies political pressure is the more likely outcome on current data.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/05/trump-warsh-fed-september-rate-hike.html

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: the Jakarta Composite closed at 6,541.38, down 0.73% on the session and down 1.18% on the week, the weakest weekly performance in the ASEAN set. The index has given back its early-September gains and its five-day normalized trend reads 98.8, below the 100 baseline, so the direction is still down even after the Friday bounce in US equities.
- 10Y government bond yield: 7.14%, flat on the latest reading and still at the top of the recent range. Direction is flat-to-upward: with the Fed priced to hike and US long-end yields biased higher, the room for Bank Indonesia to let local yields fall is limited, and any widening of the US-Indonesia spread would push the 10Y up rather than down.
- 5Y CDS: 82.85 bps, essentially unchanged and near the low end of Indonesia's multi-year range. Direction is stable, but the level is now the risk: Indonesia is priced as a low-risk credit while its main trading partners absorb an oil shock, so the CDS has more room to widen from here than to tighten further.
- News that could move the Indonesian market: three threads matter most. First, the oil channel: world oil at US$107 after the Saudi pipeline shutdown forces a choice between letting subsidised fuel prices rise into a hawkish-Fed backdrop or absorbing the cost through the budget and higher bond supply. Second, the external constraint: with the rupiah at 17,606 and the Fed close to hiking, Bank Indonesia has little room to cut, and defending the currency would drain reserves. Third, the fiscal debate: the DPR has flagged the polemic over DKI Jakarta's regional bond issuance after pushback from the Finance Ministry, a signal that sub-sovereign debt discipline is now part of the market conversation.
- Positioning implication: Indonesia screens as a low-beta credit in a high-beta environment. The combination of a 7.14% 10Y yield and 82.85 bps CDS leaves thin compensation for widening risk, so favour domestically driven, hard-currency-hedged exposure over rate-sensitive and import-cost-exposed names until the Fed and oil paths clarify.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed decision this week is the dominant catalyst: a hike is roughly 70% priced, so surprise risk is two-sided but skewed hawkish given firm core CPI; a hike plus hawkish guidance pressures the rupiah, JCI, and ASEAN rates simultaneously.
- Oil escalation is the second driver: the Hormuz bypass is gone, Red Sea shipping is under threat, and the Gulf-Iran talks were postponed, so the risk premium is structurally higher; a Brent move above $110 triggers a second round of imported inflation across ASEAN and renewed subsidy pressure in Indonesia.
- The counter-catalyst is diplomatic and has slipped: an Iran-Gulf shipping agreement on Hormuz remains the fastest way for the risk premium to unwind, but the postponement means the market must wait longer, and Trump's view that the Iran war ends soon after the midterms is a political forecast, not a policy fact.
- Demand destruction is the self-correcting mechanism: the US consumer outlook at 47.8 (-7.5% month on month, -13.2% year on year) and record diesel above $6 a gallon are early evidence that fuel costs are breaking demand, which would ultimately cap oil and ease the inflation impulse.
- Asia FX stress is the systemic watch item: Japan's record reserve drawdown after yen intervention is an early warning for carry-trade unwind; watch USD/JPY, regional reserves, and ASEAN FX volatility for the liquidity-squeeze signal.
- Indonesia-specific: fuel subsidy absorption supports the JCI near term but shifts risk to the fiscal and bond-supply outlook; the $1.4 billion Hartono-family offshore shift is a private-capital outflow signal; and the DPR scrutiny of regional bond issuance adds a governance dimension to the local-credit story.
- Trade policy remains a slow-burn negative: the US-Canada tariff escalation and China's soft import data keep protectionism and tepid Chinese demand as headwinds for ASEAN external demand into 2027.
- Data quality caveat: the PSEi and SET prints are stale (mid-July), VNINDEX failed to fetch entirely, and non-Indonesia CDS values did not return; ASEAN weekly comparisons excluding these are the reliable set.
