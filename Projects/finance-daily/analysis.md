# Executive Summary

- US equities bounced on Friday but still lost the week: S&P 500 +0.86% to 7,656.98 (-0.80% wk), Nasdaq +0.96% to 26,333.04 (-0.66% wk), Dow +0.98% to 52,573.29 (-1.57% wk). The rally is a single-input relief trade on a softer oil print, not a change in trend.
- The Fed is the dominant catalyst next week. August core CPI topped forecasts and headline CPI ran 3.4% year on year against 3.1% wage growth, pushing the priced odds of a HIKE to about 70%, the first tightening in three years. MarketWatch flags a credible path to three hikes (CNBC, Straits Times, Edge SG, MarketWatch).
- Oil remains the transmission channel from geopolitics to inflation. US crude topped $100 and Brent closed above $101 on Wednesday, the highest since May, then gave back part of the move into Friday on hopes of a diplomatic opening (CNBC, Straits Times).
- Supply risk is now structural, not headline: Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline after drone attacks from Iraq, removing the main Hormuz bypass, the Houthis advanced on a Red Sea island near Bab el-Mandeb, and the US restricted tanker air-defence windows to two slots a day through Hormuz (CNBC, FT, Edge SG).
- Product markets carry the acute stress: US diesel set a record above $6 a gallon and the IEA warned the refining system is "stretched to the limit", which is the channel that turns a crude headline into core goods inflation (CNBC).
- Asia sold off hard into the oil and yield shock and only partly recovered: Nikkei 225 -1.93% on Friday to 64,011.34 (-3.60% wk), Hang Seng -0.60% to 24,805.63 (-2.39% wk), KLCI -1.10% to 1,686.74. STI was the relative hold at +0.11% but still -1.66% on the week (Market data).
- JCI was the ASEAN laggard: 6,541.38, -0.73% on the session and -1.18% on the week. Indonesia's risk pricing stays benign but offers no compensation: 10Y yield 7.14% (flat), 5Y CDS 82.85 bps (flat), while the rupiah sits soft at 17,606 (Market data).

# Top Stories

## Fed hike odds near 70% after firm core CPI | CNBC
Insight: August core CPI came in above the 0.2% consensus and headline ran 3.4% year on year against 3.1% wage growth, so real incomes are falling and the Fed sees the case for tightening. Traders pushed September hike odds to about 70%, the first increase in three years, and MarketWatch notes the Fed historically does not stop at one.
Action: Cut duration and crowded high-multiple growth into the meeting, hold dry powder, and treat the Fed statement and dot path as the single largest risk event for ASEAN risk assets and the rupiah this week.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/cpi-inflation-report-august-2026.html

## US crude tops $100 and Brent closes above $101 on Hormuz risk | CNBC
Insight: US crude traded back above $100 for the first time since May and Brent closed above $101 midweek as the US-Iran conflict escalated. Friday's pullback snapped multi-day winning streaks, so the market is pricing a diplomatic opening, not de-escalation. For import-dependent Indonesia this flows straight into fuel subsidy cost and imported inflation.
Action: Treat Brent above $100 as the base case. Hedge energy-import exposure, model renewed fuel subsidy pressure on the 2027 budget, and stay selective on ASEAN transport and consumer discretionary.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/12/oils-roundtrip-back-to-100-why-china-could-determine-what-happens-next.html

## Saudi Arabia shuts the East-West pipeline that bypasses Hormuz | CNBC
Insight: Drone attacks from Iraq forced Saudi Arabia to shut the East-West crude pipeline, the main route that avoids the Strait of Hormuz. Removing the primary bypass materially raises market sensitivity to any Hormuz disruption, and the Houthis' advance to a Red Sea island further threatens the Bab el-Mandeb choke point.
Action: Stress-test portfolios for sustained Brent above $110. With the bypass gone, headline risk is asymmetric to the upside for energy and to the downside for Asian importers, so hedge the tail rather than chase spot.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/saudi-arabia-shut-down-east-west-crude-oil-pipeline.html

## Iran and Gulf states weigh a rare Hormuz meeting | Edge SG
Insight: Gulf states are considering a rare meeting with Iran next week on Hormuz, and Trump said he expects the Iran war to end soon after the midterms with oil prices then falling sharply. This is the main credible downside catalyst for crude and the market is pricing almost none of it.
Action: Do not add energy beta at current levels without a hedge. A credible Hormuz agreement is the fastest way for the risk premium to unwind and it would hurt oil longs hard, so structure any energy position for two-sided outcomes.
Source: https://www.theedgesingapore.com/news/geopolitics/gulf-states-weigh-rare-meeting-iran-next-week-hormuz--bloomberg

## US diesel hits a record above $6 a gallon as refining stays stretched | CNBC
Insight: Diesel, the fuel that moves agriculture, mining, and freight, set an all-time record, and the IEA warned inventories are shrinking and refineries are "stretched to the limit" as the Iran and Ukraine wars tighten supply. Product cracks, not flat crude, are where the stress is concentrated.
Action: Hedge fuel input costs for industrial and logistics exposure, expect another round of fuel surcharges across ASEAN transport and airlines, and prefer refining-margin exposure over flat crude beta.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/diesel-price-iran-strait-hormuz-ukraine-russia.html

## US consumer outlook plunges as the inflation outlook worsens | CNBC
Insight: The consumer outlook index fell to 47.8, down 7.5% from August and 13.2% from a year ago, with sentiment sliding on higher gas prices and trade tensions. Demand destruction from fuel costs is now visible in the data, which is the mechanism by which an oil shock eventually caps itself.
Action: Watch ASEAN consumer discretionary, travel, and transport exposure for downgrade risk. The demand-destruction signal is early, so keep positioning ahead of it rather than reacting after earnings cuts.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/consumer-outlook-plunges-in-september-as-inflation-outlook-worsens.html

## Japan's foreign reserves fall a record $80 billion after yen intervention | CNBC
Insight: Official reserves dropped to $1.207 trillion from $1.287 trillion in July, a record monthly drawdown, after intervention to support the yen. A record reserve burn in Asia's largest currency major signals unusual FX stress and raises carry-trade unwind risk across the region.
Action: Track USD/JPY and Japanese reserve data as an early warning for regional FX dislocation. A stronger yen plus higher US yields is the classic liquidity-squeeze combination for ASEAN risk assets, so keep FX hedges current.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/07/japan-foreign-reserves-yen-intervention.html

## China's August imports miss estimates as rebalancing pressure grows | CNBC
Insight: China's exports and imports gathered momentum in August, but imports still missed estimates, underscoring soft domestic demand and pointing to a soft commodity and component demand outlook for ASEAN exporters even where order books look stable.
Action: Prefer ASEAN names with domestic revenue drivers over China-export-sensitive industrials until Chinese import demand confirms a turn, and watch rebalancing measures for commodity demand signals.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/china-exports-imports-august-trade-rebalance-demand-surplus-.html

## Trump escalates pressure on Warsh as the Fed hike looms | CNBC
Insight: Ten days before the Fed meeting, the administration is in a full-court press to stop a hike, and Warsh faces intensifying pressure after hot CPI. Political pressure on the Fed raises the risk of a credibility shock if the committee hikes anyway or signals more to come.
Action: Price a small probability of a policy-credibility event into long-end US Treasury and USD positioning, and note that a hawkish Fed that defies political pressure is the more likely outcome on current data.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/05/trump-warsh-fed-september-rate-hike.html

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: the Jakarta Composite closed at 6,541.38, down 0.73% on the session and down 1.18% on the week, the weakest weekly performance in the ASEAN set. The index has given back its early-September gains and sits below the level implied by the 98.8 normalized reading on the five-day trend, with the decline concentrated in the sessions after the CPI print and the oil spike.
- 10Y government bond yield: 7.14%, flat on the latest reading and still at the top of the recent range. Direction is flat-to-upward: with the Fed priced to hike and US long-end yields biased higher, the room for Bank Indonesia to let local yields fall is limited, and any widening of the US-Indonesia spread would push the 10Y up rather than down.
- 5Y CDS: 82.85 bps, essentially unchanged and near the low end of Indonesia's multi-year range. Direction is stable, but the level is now the risk: Indonesia is priced as a low-risk credit while its main trading partners absorb an oil shock, so the CDS has more room to widen from here than to tighten further.
- News that could move the Indonesian market: two threads matter most. First, the fiscal channel: with oil above $100 the government must either let subsidized fuel prices rise, which feeds inflation into a hawkish-Fed backdrop, or absorb the cost through the budget, which raises bond-supply and fiscal risk into 2027. Second, the external constraint: with the rupiah at 17,606 and the Fed close to hiking, Bank Indonesia has little room to cut, and defending the currency against a hawkish Fed would drain reserves. A hawkish BI hold, a reserve drawdown signal, or an explicit subsidy expansion are the three domestic events that would move the JCI most.
- Positioning implication: Indonesia screens as a low-beta credit in a high-beta environment. The combination of a 7.14% 10Y yield and 82.85 bps CDS leaves thin compensation for widening risk, so favor domestically driven, hard-currency-hedged exposure over rate-sensitive and import-cost-exposed names until the Fed and oil paths clarify.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed decision next week is the dominant catalyst: a hike is roughly 70% priced, so surprise risk is two-sided but skewed hawkish given firm core CPI; a hike plus hawkish guidance pressures the rupiah, JCI, and ASEAN rates simultaneously.
- Oil escalation is the second driver: the Hormuz bypass is gone and Red Sea shipping is under threat, so the risk premium is structurally higher; a Brent move above $110 triggers a second round of imported inflation across ASEAN and renewed subsidy pressure in Indonesia.
- The counter-catalyst is diplomatic: an Iran-Gulf shipping agreement on Hormuz, or a credible end to the Iran war, is the single fastest way for the risk premium to unwind, and the market is pricing almost none of it. Friday's oil pullback is the first sign of that pricing.
- Demand destruction is the self-correcting mechanism: the US consumer outlook at 47.8 (-7.5% month on month, -13.2% year on year) and record diesel above $6 a gallon are early evidence that fuel costs are breaking demand, which would ultimately cap oil and ease the inflation impulse.
- Asia FX stress is the systemic watch item: Japan's record $80 billion reserve drawdown is an early warning for carry-trade unwind; watch USD/JPY, regional reserves, and ASEAN FX volatility for the liquidity-squeeze signal.
- Indonesia-specific: fuel subsidy absorption supports the JCI near term but shifts risk to the fiscal and bond-supply outlook, and the $1.4 billion Hartono-family offshore shift is a private-capital outflow signal; the 7.14% 10Y and 82.85 bps CDS leave little cushion for a widening shock.
- Trade policy remains a slow-burn negative: the US-Canada tariff escalation and China's soft import data keep protectionism and tepid Chinese demand as headwinds for ASEAN external demand into 2027.
- Data quality caveat: PSEi and SET prints are stale (mid-July), and VNINDEX failed to fetch entirely; ASEAN weekly comparisons excluding these three are the reliable set.
