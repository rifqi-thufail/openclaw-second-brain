# Executive Summary

- The US July jobs report missed badly: nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 against the +83,000 consensus, though the unemployment rate eased to 4.1%; traders scaled back rate-hike bets and the S&P 500 closed at a record 7,757.64 (+0.62%) with the Dow at 54,036.93.
- Oil extended its rebound as Iran struck targets in the Strait of Hormuz: Brent rose 1.4% to US$83.62 after a 4.9% jump the prior session, and Iran's restrictive draft plan on Hormuz traffic keeps the chokepoint risk premium volatile against repeated deal headlines.
- Trump revived his effort to fire Fed governor Lisa Cook, two months after the Supreme Court blocked it, adding governance uncertainty to a Federal Reserve already split between holding and hiking with core inflation at 3.3%.
- China's July exports surged 23.9% YoY with chip shipments nearly doubling, yet the factory PMI contracted in July; Trump extended tariffs to polysilicon products, lifting solar stocks globally.
- ASEAN was mixed: STI rose 1.03% to 5,638.99 and JCI held near 6,343.71, while KLCI fell 0.63% and Hang Seng dropped 1.49%; the Philippines reported postpandemic-low Q2 GDP growth of 2.3%.
- Indonesia stayed stable: the 10Y yield held at 7.14% for a third straight day, the 5Y CDS crept up to 90.5 bps from 90.1, and Danantara signed a US$2.5 billion protein ecosystem partnership with JBS Group.
- Positioning signals are building: gold bulls spent US$180 million on bullish calls despite a 25% drawdown from January highs as the dollar slips, and copper is heading for a record close on a tighter global market.

# Top Stories

## US unexpectedly sheds 23,000 jobs in July; S&P closes at record | The Straits Times
Insight: Nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 versus the 83,000 gain expected, while the unemployment rate eased to 4.1% from 4.2%, largely because workers left the labor force; economists call the market "slow hire, slow fire." The soft print prompted traders to scale back Fed rate-rise bets, powering the S&P 500 to a record close of 7,757.64 (+0.62%) and the Dow to 54,036.93, a direct tailwind for EM assets including the rupiah if it holds.
Action: Favor duration and EM-sensitive exposure on reduced hike odds, but keep inflation (core 3.3%) and the still-divided Fed as hedges; the September meeting is the next catalyst.

## Oil extends gains as Iran strikes targets in Strait of Hormuz | The Straits Times
Insight: Brent jumped 1.4% to US$83.62 after a 4.9% surge the prior session, as Iran hit targets in the Strait of Hormuz and published a restrictive draft plan that would bar US and Israeli ships; Washington rejected the terms and CNBC notes markets keep pouncing on deal headlines that never materialize. With Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan forming a new defense pact, the geopolitical premium in crude is rebuilding. For Indonesia, higher oil pressures the rupiah and the fuel subsidy bill while firming coal supports exporters.
Action: Keep energy exposure or hedges against Hormuz tail risk; treat oil as the swing factor for Indonesian assets, and fade headline-driven rallies in either direction until a verified deal exists.

## Trump revives effort to fire Fed governor Lisa Cook | MarketWatch
Insight: Two months after the Supreme Court blocked his first attempt, Trump resumed the push to oust governor Lisa Cook, with aide Dan Scavino alleging false statements on mortgage agreements, part of a broader campaign to stack the Fed board with allies. The move lands just after a soft jobs report and while Wall Street reads the last Fed decision as a hike likely on the horizon; it deepens uncertainty over Fed independence and rate policy, which transmits directly to USD and EM funding conditions.
Action: Monitor confirmation headlines and Fed communication closely; any perceived erosion of Fed independence is a risk to long-dated US yields and EM currencies, so keep policy-sensitive positions flexible.

## China July exports beat forecasts on high-tech demand; solar stocks shine | The Straits Times
Insight: China's exports expanded 23.9% YoY in July with chip shipments nearly doubling, a counterpoint to the contractionary factory PMI, and Trump extended US tariffs to polysilicon products, sending solar stocks higher in premarket trade. The export strength supports ASEAN tech supply chains, while the polysilicon tariff redirects solar module value chains and raises costs for downstream manufacturers in the region.
Action: Hold exporters levered to Chinese high-tech demand; watch polysilicon tariff implementation for solar supply chain winners and losers across ASEAN.

## Philippines Q2 GDP grows just 2.3%, postpandemic low; reserves near two-year low | Inquirer
Insight: Philippine GDP grew only 2.3% in Q2, down from 2.8% in Q1, as the Middle East war peaked and government spending stayed weak, while gross international reserves fell to US$103.4 billion, a near two-year low, on offshore debt payments and peso intervention. Bank lending to MSMEs is stuck at 4.48% of loans, and PSBank's H1 net income dropped 39.8% on higher provisions; DMCI's 26% profit jump on nickel is a rare bright spot. The peso and sovereign credit metrics warrant close watching.
Action: Stay cautious on Philippine duration and bank exposure; prefer commodity-linked and consumer-staple names, and watch the BSP policy response to the growth-reserves squeeze.

- ID: JCI eased 0.12% to 6,343.71 but is up about 1.7% from July 31, holding the post-rotation uptrend; the 10Y yield stayed at 7.14% and the Danantara-JBS US$2.5 billion deal dominated domestic corporate headlines.

- TH: SET index data is insufficient for this cycle; the week's dominant regional news is a school shooting in Nonthaburi, with limited market-moving economic headlines, and Bangkok's recovery remains hostage to oil-driven inflation.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI closed at 6,343.71 on Aug 6, down 0.12% on the day but up roughly 1.7% over the five-session window (normalized 100 to 101.7), holding its recent uptrend after foreign rotation into state banks last week.
- The 10Y government bond yield held at 7.14% for a third consecutive session, flat across Aug 6, 7 and 8, with no material domestic rate signal this week.
- The 5Y CDS widened slightly to 90.49 bps from 90.1 bps, a mild drift up but still far from stress levels; monitor whether oil at US$83.62 and global Fed uncertainty push it toward 95 bps.
- Market-moving watch: Brent rising toward US$84 on Hormuz escalation pressures the rupiah and the fuel subsidy bill, the Danantara-JBS US$2.5 billion protein partnership signals active sovereign deal flow, and US Fed governance noise (Trump versus Lisa Cook) keeps EM rate expectations fluid; the BI governor succession remains the key domestic policy watch item.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Risk: Hormuz escalation. Iran struck targets and published a restrictive draft plan; Brent is at US$83.62 and rising, deal headlines create two-way whipsaws, and the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan defense pact adds geopolitical complexity.
- Risk: Fed governance. Trump's revived attempt to fire governor Lisa Cook follows a Supreme Court block and lands amid a divided Fed; any perceived erosion of independence is a negative for long-end yields and EM currencies.
- Risk: Russia oil sanctions. The Senate-passed bill targeting Russian oil purchasers adds a supply-side risk to an already tight crude market.
- Risk: Philippine macro stress. Sub-3% GDP growth, GIR at a near two-year low and weak MSME lending raise peso and sovereign credit questions.
- Risk: Japan FX. The yen surrendered nearly half its gains from US-Japan intervention, trading near 158.37, raising the odds of renewed intervention and regional volatility.
- Catalyst: Soft US jobs. The 23,000 payroll decline scaled back rate-hike bets and drove US indices to records, a supportive backdrop for EM and Indonesian assets if sustained.
- Catalyst: China exports. July exports rose 23.9% YoY with chip shipments nearly doubling, a tailwind for ASEAN electronics supply chains despite a soft PMI.
- Catalyst: Commodity strength. Copper is heading for a record close (+14% in 2026), gold bulls put US$180 million to work, and polysilicon tariffs reshuffle solar supply chains.
