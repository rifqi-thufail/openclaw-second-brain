# Executive Summary

- US equities snapped a four-day slide on Friday: S&P 500 +0.86% to 7,656.98, Nasdaq +0.96% to 26,333.04, Dow +0.98% to 52,573.29, but all three still finished the week lower (S&P -0.80%, Nasdaq -0.66%, Dow -1.57%).
- The macro driver flips to the Fed: August CPI came in firm and core topped forecasts, pushing the priced odds of a rate HIKE at next week's meeting to about 70%, the first tightening in three years (CNBC, Straits Times, Edge SG).
- Oil is the transmission channel. Brent closed above $101 on Wednesday, its highest since May, and US crude topped $100 again as the US-Iran conflict and Houthi advances threaten Hormuz and Red Sea flows; Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline that bypasses Hormuz (CNBC, FT).
- Diesel is the acute stress point: US diesel hit a record $6 a gallon, and the IEA warns the global refining system is "stretched to the limit" (FT, CNBC, IEA).
- Asia sold off hard into the oil and yield shock: Nikkei -3.0% on Friday intraday, Kospi -2.7%, Hang Seng -1.27% to 24,954.47 and STI -0.70% to 5,689.75; global bonds fell as inflation risk repriced (Straits Times, CNBC).
- JCI was the regional underperformer into the print: 6,589.34, -1.33% on the session and -0.71% on the week, the weakest ASEAN weekly move, with the rupiah soft (USD/IDR 17,606, +0.60% on the day, -0.18% on the week).
- Indonesia risk pricing stays benign but is no longer improving: 10Y yield at 7.14%, 5Y CDS at 82.85 bps, a low sovereign-risk signal that sits uncomfortably against a hawkish Fed and heavy oil.

# Top Stories

## Fed hike odds near 70% after firm core CPI | CNBC
Insight: The all-items CPI was expected at +0.4% in August with core at +0.2%, but core came in above forecast, and traders moved the probability of a September increase to roughly 70%. PPI rose 0.4% as expected but with firmer details. This is the first credible tightening cycle restart in three years and it inverts the rate-relief trade that has supported duration and high-multiple equities.
Action: Cut duration and high-multiple growth into the meeting, hold dry powder, and treat any Fed statement as the week's single largest risk event for ASEAN risk assets and the rupiah.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/cpi-inflation-report-august-2026.html

## Brent closes above $101 and WTI tops $100 on Hormuz and Red Sea risk | CNBC
Insight: Brent closed above $101, its highest since May, and US crude topped $100 again as the US-Iran conflict escalated and reports of strikes on Saudi Aramco-linked infrastructure added risk premium. Diesel in the US topped $6 a gallon, a record, and the IEA warned refining capacity is "stretched to the limit". For import-dependent Indonesia this is a direct hit to fuel subsidy cost and imported inflation.
Action: Treat Brent above $100 as the base case. Hedge energy-import exposure, expect renewed fuel subsidy pressure on the Indonesian budget, and be selective on ASEAN transport and consumer discretionary.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-us-iran-hormuz-attacks.html

## Saudi Arabia shuts the East-West pipeline that bypasses Hormuz after drone attacks | FT
Insight: Attacks by drones from Iraq forced Saudi Arabia to shut the East-West crude pipeline, the main route that avoids the Strait of Hormuz. Removing the primary bypass materially raises the market's sensitivity to any Hormuz disruption, and Riyadh already confirmed the move puts further momentum behind fast-rising oil prices.
Action: Stress-test portfolios for a sustained Brent above $110; the loss of the bypass means headline risk is now asymmetric to the upside for energy and to the downside for Asian importers.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/3bafb3ae-ba94-44f0-984c-ec7c9f54a5e0

## Houthis seize Red Sea islands in a lightning offensive | FT
Insight: The Houthis advanced against Saudi-backed forces and cemented control over a vital shipping artery, further threatening the oil choke point and Suez-linked trade lanes. Shipping and insurance costs rise first, then freight and landed input costs for all of Asia.
Action: Add freight and marine insurance cost inflation to the 4Q model set; overweight shipping and tanker names as the direct beneficiaries and underweight import-heavy, margin-thin retail.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/976fa0d6-ed09-4a6f-be02-ff4317b45f40

## Iran and Gulf states to meet in a push for a Hormuz deal | FT
Insight: Regional states hope a temporary shipping agreement between Iran and Oman can offer a pathway to ease hostilities and reopen the strait. This is the main credible downside catalyst for oil, and the market is currently pricing almost none of it.
Action: Do not add energy beta at current levels without a hedge; a credible Hormuz agreement is the fastest way to unwind the risk premium and it would hit oil longs hard.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/df2d9bcc-59f9-4122-aa4d-2020bef77e0e

## Global bonds fall as surging oil inflames inflation risk | Straits Times
Insight: Asian stocks and bonds fell together as surging energy costs, inflation fears, and Fed hike bets combined; the Nikkei fell 2.8% and the Kospi 2.7% in the session cited. Equities and bonds selling off in tandem is the signature of an inflation shock, not a growth shock.
Action: Avoid the reflexive "buy the dip in duration" trade; in an oil-driven inflation shock the correct hedge is commodity-linked exposure and short-duration credit, not long government bonds.
Source: https://www.straitstimes.com/business/global-bonds-fall-as-surging-oil-prices-inflame-inflation-risks

## US stocks rally as the oil decline offers relief after CPI | Edge SG
Insight: Friday's equity bounce came as oil gave back some gains after the CPI release, with both Brent and WTI snapping multi-day winning streaks. The rally is therefore a relief trade on one input, not a change in the inflation or policy trend.
Action: Treat Friday's +0.86% S&P print as a tactical bounce inside a hawkish week; use strength to reduce crowded AI and long-duration positions rather than chase.
Source: https://www.theedgesingapore.com/news/us-stocks/us-stocks-rally-oil-decline-offers-relief-after-cpi-release

## US consumer outlook plunges in September as the inflation outlook worsens | CNBC
Insight: The survey's headline index fell to 47.8, down 7.5% from August and 13.2% from a year ago, while a separate read showed sentiment sliding on higher gas prices and trade tensions. Demand destruction from fuel costs is now visible in the data, which is the mechanism by which an oil shock eventually caps itself.
Action: Watch consumer discretionary and travel exposure for downgrade risk; the demand-destruction signal is early, so keep positioning on the right side of it.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/consumer-outlook-plunges-in-september-as-inflation-outlook-worsens.html

## Japan's foreign reserves drop a record $80 billion in August after yen intervention | CNBC
Insight: Official reserves fell to $1.207 trillion from $1.287 trillion in July, a record monthly drawdown, after intervention to support the yen. A record reserve burn in Asia's largest currency major signals unusual FX stress and raises carry-trade unwind risk across the region.
Action: Track USD/JPY and Japanese reserve data as an early warning for regional FX dislocation; a stronger yen plus higher US yields is the classic liquidity-squeeze combination for ASEAN risk assets.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/07/japan-foreign-reserves-yen-intervention.html

## China's August imports miss estimates as calls to rebalance trade grow | CNBC
Insight: China's exports and imports gathered momentum in August, but imports still missed estimates, underscoring soft domestic demand as pressure grows to rebalance trade. For ASEAN commodity and component exporters this softens the demand outlook even where order books look stable.
Action: Prefer ASEAN names with domestic revenue drivers over China-export-sensitive industrials until Chinese import demand confirms a turn.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/08/china-exports-imports-august-trade-rebalance-demand-surplus-.html

## US diesel hits a record $6 a gallon on the Iran supply shock | FT
Insight: Diesel is the fuel that moves agriculture, mining, and heavy industry, so a record US price feeds directly into producer costs and freight rates globally. This is the channel that turns an oil headline into core goods inflation, which is precisely what the Fed is reacting to.
Action: Hedge fuel input costs for any industrial or logistics exposure, and expect another round of fuel surcharges and tariff pass-through in ASEAN transport and airlines.
Source: https://www.ft.com/content/ebfa69a3-323b-450d-8bfd-0b12e01d2a5b

## US producer prices rise 0.4% in August with firmer details | Yahoo Finance
Insight: PPI matched the 0.4% consensus but the underlying details came in firmer, reinforcing the CPI message that pipeline inflation is not easing. Producer prices lead consumer prices by roughly one to two quarters, so this argues against any near-term disinflation case.
Action: Push out the timing of any expected policy easing into 2027 and price ASEAN rate-sensitive sectors on a higher-for-longer path.
Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/us-producer-prices-increase-expected-130400685.html

## IEA warns the global oil refining system is "stretched to the limit" | CNBC
Insight: The IEA flagged shrinking inventories and strained refineries as the Iran and Ukraine wars tighten the market, which caps how fast product prices can respond to crude moves. Product cracks, especially diesel, lead crude in this regime.
Action: Trade product cracks rather than flat crude where possible, and treat refining margin strength as the most durable energy exposure in the current setup.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/iran-war-oil-diesel-iea-hormuz.html

- Thailand: No index data is available this run: the SET print is stale (last updated mid-July, 1,639.04, +0.23%), so it is reported for completeness only and excluded from the weekly read. Oil-import exposure and tourism-linked fuel costs are the two variables to watch (Market data, stale).
- Regional cross-current: the broad ASEAN tape is being driven by two forces pulling in opposite directions. Hawkish Fed pricing and higher US yields pressure ASEAN currencies and rate-sensitive sectors, while the oil shock is a positive for Malaysian and Indonesian energy-linked names and a negative for the Philippines, Thailand and Vietnam on import costs.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: the Jakarta Composite closed at 6,589.34, down 1.33% on the session and down 0.71% on the week, the weakest weekly performance in the ASEAN set. The index gave back its early-September gains and is now below the level implied by the 99.3 normalized reading on the five-day trend chart, with the decline concentrated in the session after the CPI print and the oil spike.
- 10Y government bond yield: 7.14%, unchanged on the latest reading and still at the top of the recent range. Direction is flat-to-upward: with the Fed priced to hike and US long-end yields rising, the room for Bank Indonesia to let local yields fall is limited, and any widening of the US-Indonesia spread would put upward pressure on the 10Y.
- 5Y CDS: 82.85 bps, essentially unchanged and still near the low end of Indonesia's multi-year range. Direction is stable, but the level is now the risk: Indonesia is priced as a low-risk credit while its main trading partners face an oil shock, so the CDS has more room to widen from here than to tighten further.
- News that could move the Indonesian market: two threads matter most. First, the fuel subsidy and fiscal channel: the government published updated nationwide fuel prices effective 12 September and the energy minister publicly defended Russian crude imports against foreign pressure, both of which signal that the oil shock is being absorbed through the budget rather than passed fully to consumers. A subsidy expansion would support the JCI in the short run but raise fiscal and bond-supply risk into 2027. Second, the external constraint: with the Fed close to hiking and the rupiah at 17,606 (soft, +0.60% on the day against the dollar), Bank Indonesia has little room to cut, and any attempt to defend the rupiah against a hawkish Fed would drain reserves. A surprise BI hold with hawkish language, or any sign of reserve drawdown, is the single largest domestic risk event on the horizon.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed decision next week is the dominant catalyst: a hike is roughly 70% priced, so the surprise risk is two-sided but skewed hawkish given firm core CPI and PPI; a hike plus hawkish guidance pressures the rupiah, JCI, and ASEAN rates simultaneously.
- Oil escalation: loss of the Hormuz bypass (East-West pipeline shut) plus Houthi control of Red Sea islands means the risk premium is structurally higher; a Brent move above $110 is the trigger for a second round of imported inflation across ASEAN.
- The counter-catalyst is diplomatic: an Iran-Gulf shipping agreement on Hormuz is the single fastest way for the risk premium to unwind, and the market is pricing almost none of it.
- Demand destruction: US consumer outlook at 47.8 (-7.5% month on month, -13.2% year on year) and record diesel at $6 a gallon are the first visible signs that fuel costs are breaking demand, which would ultimately cap the oil move and ease the inflation impulse.
- Asia FX stress: Japan's record $80 billion reserve drawdown is an early warning for carry-trade unwind; watch USD/JPY, regional reserves, and any widening in ASEAN FX volatility for the liquidity-squeeze signal.
- Indonesia-specific: fuel subsidy absorption supports the JCI near term but shifts risk to the fiscal and bond-supply outlook; the combination of a 7.14% 10Y and 82.85 bps CDS leaves little compensation for the widening bias.
- Data quality caveat: PSEi and SET prints are stale (mid-July), and VNINDEX failed to fetch entirely; ASEAN weekly comparisons excluding these are the reliable set.
- Trade policy: the US-Canada tariff escalation and China's soft import data keep protectionism and tepid Chinese demand as slow-burn negatives for ASEAN external demand.
