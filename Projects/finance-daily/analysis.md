# Executive Summary

- US equities ended a volatile week higher but narrowly. The S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq gained 0.39% to 26,522.54 on 18 September, while the Dow fell 0.18% to 51,682.64. Gains are concentrated in technology; cyclicals lag.
- The weekly tape is weaker than the daily print. The S&P 500 is up 0.40% but the Dow is down 1.41%, confirming a narrow, AI-led rally rather than broad risk appetite.
- The Fed dominates the macro outlook. Chair Kevin Warsh's first hike is done, but his remarks left the forward path open, and the market is now debating the terminal rate. This is the largest transmission channel into emerging market asset prices.
- Oil finished the week flat. Saudi Arabia restarted the damaged East-West pipeline within days and offered more crude through Hormuz, so the supply-shock premium unwound without breaking the inflation impulse.
- Asia diverged from the US. The Nikkei led at 65,018.95 (+1.38% on the day, +2.40% on the week), the Hang Seng added 0.60%, while the STI (-0.08%), KLCI (-0.55%) and JCI (-0.33%) closed lower.
- Indonesia's external indicators pull in opposite directions. The 10Y government bond yield held at 7.14%, the 5Y CDS eased to 81.78 bps, but USD/IDR remains weak at 17,735 with a 0.56% weekly rise.
- ASEAN quantitative coverage is incomplete. The SET, PSEi and VN-Index were skipped again for insufficient price data, and non-Indonesian CDS peers remain unavailable. Treat these as data gaps, not neutral signals.

# Top Stories

## Three words from Kevin Warsh have Wall Street wondering how far the Fed will go with rate hikes | CNBC Top
Insight: Chair Warsh used his first post-decision remarks to justify this week's rate increase while leaving the forward path open, re-opening the debate on the terminal rate. The long end of the US Treasury curve is still expected to trade at elevated yields. This is the single largest transmission channel into Indonesian bonds, the rupiah and ASEAN equity multiples.
Action: Trade the front end on data, not rhetoric. Keep US Treasury duration short, and within EM local debt prefer markets with improving credit (Indonesia CDS at 81.78 bps) over those with wide current-account deficits. Reassess after the next CPI release.

## Oil finishes the week flat as the market sees Saudi pipeline outage as less disruptive than feared | CNBC Asia
Insight: Crude ended the week broadly unchanged after Saudi Arabia offered additional supply through the Strait of Hormuz and the US said the damaged East-West pipeline would restart within days. Continued Saudi-Houthi cross-border strikes were shrugged off, which means the geopolitical risk premium is being priced as temporary rather than structural. Flat-to-lower oil directly relieves fuel subsidy budgets in Indonesia and reduces the Philippines' import bill.
Action: Keep a small, cheap upside hedge because the supply cushion remains thin, but do not underwrite sustained triple-digit crude. In regional equities, treat lower oil as a positive for Indonesian and Philippine domestic demand names and a mild negative for upstream energy earnings.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-saudi-arabia-houthi.html

## S&P 500, Nasdaq advance, turning the page on a tumultuous week | Straits Times
Insight: The S&P 500 gained 12.74 points, or 0.17%, to 7,650.50, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64. The divergence is the story: strength is concentrated in technology and AI-linked names, while industrials and cyclicals lag. A 0.40% weekly gain after a Fed hike tells you the market is treating tightening as priced rather than incremental.
Action: Keep portfolio beta in quality growth with visible AI-linked revenue. Avoid broad cyclical reflation trades until the September CPI print confirms the inflation trajectory, and use the Dow's relative weakness as a signal on industrial and consumer exposure.

## PH seen facing bigger currency pressure from hawkish Fed | Inquirer PH
Insight: ANZ warns that Asian economies with wide current-account deficits and limited benefit from the AI boom, specifically the Philippines, are most sensitive to expectations of higher US rates. Rising Treasury yields, elevated oil and a weak peso have already hit Philippine asset appetite. The Philippines is the clearest live demonstration of how Fed tightening transmits into ASEAN risk assets.
Action: Underweight Philippine equities versus Indonesia, Singapore and Vietnam until the peso stabilises and the BSP's response is clarified. Watch the next BSP policy statement for whether it defends the currency or prioritises growth: a defensive hike would pressure domestic cyclicals further.

## Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire chairman, replaced by son Howard | CNBC Top
Insight: Buffett handed the Berkshire chair to his son Howard after building a failing textile mill into a US$1.1 trillion conglomerate. This is a governance milestone for the largest US conglomerate: succession removes the key-man premium that has historically supported the valuation multiple and shifts attention to capital allocation under the successor structure. Berkshire's cash balance and buyback policy become the swing factor for the shares.
Action: Expect near-term volatility in Berkshire and in companies where a Buffett stake has been read as a quality endorsement. Do not treat the announcement as a broader market signal; the succession risk is contained to the issuer and its closest comparables.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/19/warren-buffett-takes-next-step-in-plan-to-keep-berkshire-from-straying.html

## Investors warn Anthropic could struggle to sustain revenues post-IPO | FT Home

SoftBank separately added US$21 billion of fresh debt capacity, including a larger Arm margin loan, to fund AI infrastructure. The AI capital cycle's funding stress point is now visible: equity markets are being asked to absorb increasingly large infrastructure issuance while anchor demand stories remain private.
Action: Treat AI infrastructure credit as the emerging risk concentration of the cycle. Prefer AI supply-chain equity exposure (semiconductors, test equipment, data-centre operators with contracted revenue) over unsecured data-centre debt, and monitor AI-linked IPO outcomes as a sentiment marker for the complex.

Insight: The abrupt transition at the top of Indonesia's finance ministry leaves the deficit path and policy continuity unresolved, and the market is waiting for the first substantive policy signal. The rupiah at 17,735 is the clearest expression of that uncertainty. Crucially, the 5Y CDS eased to 81.78 bps while the currency weakened, which means foreign credit markets are not yet pricing a fiscal credibility problem.

Watch the next government bond auction for bid-to-cover and foreign participation, and read the new minister's first policy statements for guidance on the deficit ceiling. A weak auction validates further rupiah depreciation; a strong one makes the current yield level attractive.

# Indonesia Focus

- JCI level and trend: The JCI closed at 6,441.16, down 0.33% on the day and 1.43% over the week, its fourth consecutive weekly decline. The index has fallen from 6,541.38 on 15 September to 6,441.16 on 18 September, and the rebound attempt on 17 September (6,461.15) failed. The trend is still down; sustained recovery requires foreign flows to return, which depends on the currency stabilising.
- 10Y government bond yield direction: The 10-year Indonesian government bond yield stands at 7.14%, unchanged across the last five sessions (15 to 19 September). A completely flat yield through a week that included a US Fed hike is strong evidence that domestic demand is absorbing supply and that the market is not repricing Indonesian credit risk. The risk case remains a delayed repricing: if US long-end yields extend higher, the next auction could clear at a higher yield.
- 5Y CDS level and direction: The 5Y Indonesia CDS is 81.78 bps, down from 82.19 bps the prior session and 83.74 bps a week ago. The direction is consistently positive (83.69, 84.48, 83.74, 82.19, 81.78 over the last five sessions) and the absolute level is low by historical EM standards. Foreign credit markets are not pricing a fiscal credibility problem. The divergence between improving CDS and a weakening rupiah (17,735, +0.56% on the week) is the key signal to monitor: if CDS turns higher while the rupiah keeps falling, that would be a genuine stress repricing rather than a liquidity-driven currency move.
- News that could move the Indonesian market significantly: The finance ministry transition remains the dominant domestic catalyst. Market attention is fixed on the new minister's first substantive policy statements and the next government bond auction for bid-to-cover and foreign participation. Oil's flat weekly finish is a material offsetting positive for Indonesia's external balance and fuel subsidy budget. Separately, President Prabowo's disclosure of a plan to produce gasoline from coal adds a long-horizon energy policy signal, and the US tariff legislation enabling duties on buyers of Russian oil carries indirect risk for Indonesian commodity exports if the scope broadens.

# Risks & Catalysts

- Fed follow-through: Chair Warsh left the tightening path open. A second consecutive hike would lift US yields, pressure Indonesian bonds and the rupiah simultaneously, and compress EM equity multiples. This is the highest-probability systemic risk in the current setup.
- Rupiah depreciation: USD/IDR at 17,735 with a 0.56% weekly rise and a fifth consecutive session of weakness. The CDS-yield-rupiah divergence cannot persist indefinitely; if the currency breaks materially faster, Bank Indonesia faces a choice between defensive rate action and the government's growth agenda.
- Indonesia fiscal credibility: The finance ministry transition is unresolved at the policy level. The next bond auction and the new minister's first policy statements are the tests. Loss of credibility would show first in the rupiah, then in CDS.
- Oil reversal: The supply cushion remains thin despite the pipeline restart and Saudi rerouting through Hormuz. Renewed Saudi-Houthi escalation or a failed restart would revive the inflation impulse and hit Indonesia and the Philippines hardest.
- AI infrastructure funding: Anthropic's post-IPO revenue durability concerns, SoftBank's US$21 billion of new debt and the broader infrastructure issuance wave are the same risk from different angles. A funding squeeze in AI infrastructure would hit Singapore, Malaysia and Vietnam supply chains disproportionately and would be the first cyclical crack in the strongest regional growth engine.
- Philippine currency and equity risk: A wide current-account deficit, elevated oil and an already weak peso leave the Philippines the most Fed-sensitive ASEAN economy per ANZ. A disorderly peso move would force BSP action and pressure domestic demand.
- US trade policy: The US House has enabled tariffs of up to 100% on buyers of Russian oil, giving the administration new leverage over China and India, with knock-on effects on ASEAN export demand and supply-chain reconfiguration.
- Data gaps: The SET (Thailand), PSEi (Philippines) and VN-Index (Vietnam) indices were skipped due to insufficient price data, and ASEAN CDS peers (MY, TH, PH, VN, SG) remain unavailable. Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines are therefore covered qualitatively only in the quantitative sections.
